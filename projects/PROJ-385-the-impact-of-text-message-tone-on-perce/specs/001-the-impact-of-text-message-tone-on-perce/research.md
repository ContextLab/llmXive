# Research: The Impact of Text Message Tone on Perceived Emotional Support

## Executive Summary

This research investigates the moderating role of relational context (close friend vs. acquaintance) on the perceived emotional support of text messages containing varying paralinguistic cues. We hypothesize that the effect of specific cues (emoji, punctuation, length) on perceived support is stronger in close friend relationships than in acquaintance relationships. The study employs a within-subjects factorial design manipulating Relationship, Emoji, Punctuation, and Length.

## Theoretical Background

### Paralinguistic Cues in Text-Based Communication
Text messaging lacks non-verbal cues (tone of voice, facial expression). Users employ "paralinguistic digital affordances" to compensate:
*   **Emojis**: Often serve to clarify emotional intent and soften tone.
*   **Punctuation**: Excessive punctuation (e.g., "!!!") can signal heightened emotional intensity or urgency.
*   **Length**: Longer messages may imply greater effort and sincerity.

### Relational Context as a Moderator
The interpretation of these cues is mediated by the relationship:
*   **Close Friends**: High shared context allows for nuanced interpretation. Cues might be seen as playful or deeply supportive.
*   **Acquaintances**: Low shared context may lead to ambiguity. Excessive cues might be perceived as insincere or aggressive.

## Methodology

### Study Design
A 2 (Relationship: Close Friend vs. Acquaintance) × 3 (Emoji: 0, 1, 2+) × 2 (Punctuation: Standard, Excessive) × 2 (Length: Short, Long) within-subjects factorial design.
*   **Stimuli**: Text messages generated by systematically combining the above levels (12 unique variants).
*   **Participants**: N ≥ 60 unique adults (18-35), recruited via Prolific.
*   **Task**: Rate perceived emotional support on a 7-point Likert scale (1=Not at all, 7=Extremely).
*   **Power Analysis**: N=60 is assumed sufficient to detect the primary interaction (Relationship × Cue Intensity composite) with acceptable power, acknowledging the limitation for detecting smaller effects in the full 12-condition factorial. The 2x2 power analysis (Relationship x Composite) was used as a heuristic for the primary hypothesis, while the full design is exploratory for specific cue effects.

### Statistical Analysis Plan

#### Primary Model: Linear Mixed-Effects Model (LMM)
To account for the hierarchical structure (multiple ratings per participant, multiple ratings per stimulus), we will use an LMM.
*   **Fixed Effects**:
    1.  **Relationship** (Categorical: Friend vs. Acquaintance)
    2.  **Emoji** (Ordinal: 0, 1, 2+)
    3.  **Punctuation** (Categorical: Standard vs. Excessive)
    4.  **Length** (Categorical: Short vs. Long)
    5.  **Interaction Terms**: Relationship × Emoji, Relationship × Punctuation, Relationship × Length.
    6.  **Composite Cue Intensity**: A derived continuous score (for exploratory comparison only).
*   **Random Effects**: Random intercepts for `Participant_ID` and `Stimulus_ID`. The design is fully crossed (each participant rates all 12 stimuli in both contexts), justifying the random intercept for Stimulus. Stimulus IDs are unique per item (S-S012).
*   **Estimation**: Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML).
*   **Degrees of Freedom**: Satterthwaite approximation.
*   **Software**: `statsmodels` (Python) via CPU-only execution.
*   **Primary Hypothesis**: The interaction of Relationship with individual cues (Emoji, Punctuation, Length) will be significant, indicating that the effect of these cues depends on the relational context.

#### Sensitivity Analysis
To address the arbitrariness of defining "Cue Intensity," we will:
1.  Run the primary model with **individual cues** as predictors (as above).
2.  Run secondary models with **pre-aggregated 'Cue Intensity'** scores (using Equal, Emoji-Dominant, and Punctuation-Dominant weightings).
3.  Compare the **model fit** (AIC/BIC) of the individual-cue models versus the aggregated models to determine the best-fitting cue structure.
*   **Metric**: Consistency of the interaction effect direction and significance across models, and model selection criteria (AIC/BIC) to identify the most parsimonious explanation. This avoids circular validation by testing the robustness of the *individual cue* findings against alternative model specifications, rather than testing the stability of an arbitrary aggregate index.

#### Post-Hoc Analysis
If the interaction of Relationship with any individual cue is significant (p < 0.05), Tukey-corrected pairwise comparisons will be performed to identify specific differences between cue levels within each relationship context.

## Risk Assessment

1.  **Recruitment Failure**: If <60 participants are recruited, power is insufficient.
    *   *Mitigation*: Project halts; re-evaluation of scope required.
2.  **Straight-lining**: Participants rating all items identically.
    *   *Mitigation*: Automated detection (variance=0) and exclusion.
3.  **Missing Data**: Participants dropping out.
    *   *Mitigation*: Listwise deletion or LMM handling of missingness.
4.  **Cue Intensity Definition**: Arbitrary cutoffs.
    *   *Mitigation*: Sensitivity analysis comparing individual-cue models to aggregated models using AIC/BIC to demonstrate robustness and identify the best-fitting structure.
