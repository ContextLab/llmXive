## Unresolved concerns

- The plan sets a hard threshold N >= 30 for hypothesis testing. While this is a common rule of thumb, it lacks a formal power analysis. For a Spearman correlation, N=30 provides only ~50% power to detect a moderate effect size (r=0.3) at alpha=0.05. The plan should either justify this threshold with a power calculation (e.g., 'We aim for 80% power to detect r=0.45') or explicitly state that the study is underpowered for small-to-moderate effects and interpret non-significant results with extreme caution. Relying solely on N=30 without power justification risks a high false-negative rate.
