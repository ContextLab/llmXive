# Neural Correlates of Simulated Social Exclusion on Default Mode Network Dynamics

**Field**: neuroscience

## Research question

How does acute simulated social exclusion modulate the dynamic functional connectivity of the default mode network (DMN) in healthy adults, as measured with publicly available task‑based fMRI data?

## Motivation

Social exclusion reliably triggers adverse mental‑health outcomes, yet the neural pathways that translate this acute threat into altered brain network dynamics remain unclear. The DMN is central to self‑referential processing, and characterizing its state‑dependent connectivity during exclusion could distinguish transient stress responses from trait‑level abnormalities observed in clinical populations. Providing empirical evidence of DMN modulation will inform models of social pain and guide interventions for disorders marked by heightened rejection sensitivity.

## Related work

- [Classification of Alzheimer's Dementia vs. Healthy subjects by studying structural disparities in fMRI Time‑Series of DMN (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19990) — Demonstrates how DMN ROI time‑series can be extracted from fMRI and used for quantitative connectivity analyses, establishing a methodological precedent for our planned DMN functional‑connectivity pipeline.

## Expected results

We anticipate a statistically reliable reduction in DMN functional‑connectivity strength (e.g., lower mean Pearson correlation among PCC, mPFC, and angular gyrus) during exclusion blocks compared with inclusion blocks. Confirmation will be based on a within‑subject paired test yielding a p‑value < 0.05 and an effect size (Cohen’s d) that exceeds a small‑practical‑significance threshold, indicating a genuine state‑level modulation rather than noise.

## Methodology sketch

- **Data acquisition**: Download the preprocessed Cyberball task dataset `ds000030` (or any equivalent OpenNeuro dataset containing inclusion/exclusion blocks) from https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds000030. Verify that the total download fits the 14 GB SSD limit.
- **Preprocessing verification**: Use the provided preprocessed NIfTI files (realigned, normalized, smoothed). No additional resampling or synthetic preprocessing will be performed.
- **ROI definition**: Apply the Harvard‑Oxford cortical atlas to extract BOLD time‑series from three canonical DMN nodes: posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), and left/right angular gyrus. Extraction is performed with Nilearn’s `NiftiLabelsMasker`.
- **Condition segmentation**: Parse the task’s `events.tsv` file to isolate time points belonging to the “Inclusion” and “Exclusion” conditions for each participant, ensuring exact alignment with the recorded BOLD volumes.
- **Connectivity computation**: For each condition, compute Pearson correlation matrices among the selected DMN ROIs using the real measured time‑series. Derive a single connectivity strength metric per condition by averaging the absolute correlation values across all ROI pairs.
- **Statistical testing**: Conduct a non‑parametric paired permutation test (10 000 permutations) comparing exclusion‑ versus inclusion‑condition connectivity strengths across subjects. The null hypothesis is that the two condition distributions are identical.
- **Quality control**: Compute framewise displacement for each run; exclude any participant with > 3 mm mean displacement to avoid motion‑driven artefacts. Document the number of subjects retained after QC.
- **Visualization & reporting**: Produce condition‑wise heatmaps of ROI correlation matrices and a bar plot of mean connectivity strength with 95 % confidence intervals derived from the permutation distribution. Save all figures as PNG files for downstream inclusion.
