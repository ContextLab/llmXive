## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check
**Verdict**: pass
The question explicitly asks about the modulation of a biological phenomenon (DMN functional connectivity dynamics) by a psychological state (acute social exclusion). It does not frame the inquiry around the performance, architecture, or resource constraints of a specific computational method, but rather uses fMRI as the measurement tool for a substantive neuroscientific relationship.

### Circularity check
**Verdict**: pass
The predictor is the experimental condition (Inclusion vs. Exclusion blocks derived from the task design file), and the predicted variable is the functional connectivity strength derived from BOLD time-series data. These are independent sources: the condition is an external manipulation of social context, while the connectivity metric is a summary of the brain's physiological response to that manipulation.

### Triviality check
**Verdict**: pass
While the general link between social pain and brain activity is established, the specific quantification of *dynamic* connectivity changes within the DMN during acute exclusion remains under-specified. A null result would be informative by suggesting the DMN is robust to acute social threat or that the effect is localized to other networks, while a positive result would refine the mechanistic understanding of self-referential processing disruption.

### Question-narrowing check
**Verdict**: pass
The question names a clear domain relationship: how a specific social stressor (exclusion) affects a specific neural system (DMN). It does not masquerade as a domain question while actually asking about the feasibility of a specific algorithm or the speed of data processing.

### Overall verdict
**Verdict**: validated
All checks pass; the research question targets a genuine gap in the neurobiological understanding of social exclusion and is framed as a substantive inquiry into brain-behavior relationships rather than a methodological benchmark. The project is ready to advance to initialization.
