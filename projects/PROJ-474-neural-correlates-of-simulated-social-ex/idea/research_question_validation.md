## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass  

The question asks how an acute psychosocial event (simulated social exclusion) influences a brain network property (dynamic functional connectivity of the DMN). It is framed as a scientific inquiry about a neural phenomenon, independent of any particular analysis pipeline or computational method.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass  

The predictor is the condition (exclusion vs. inclusion) and the outcome is the DMN connectivity measured from BOLD time‑series. These are distinct data sources: the experimental manipulation versus the observed neural signal, so the relationship is not mechanically guaranteed.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass  

Both a significant reduction in DMN connectivity during exclusion and a null finding would provide novel insight: a reduction would support theories of DMN disengagement under social threat, while a null result would suggest robustness of DMN dynamics to short‑term social stress, a question not settled in the literature.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass  

The question names a domain relationship (“How does acute simulated social exclusion modulate DMN dynamics?”) rather than imposing constraints on the analytical method, hardware, or runtime.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated  

All four checks pass, indicating the research question is a well‑posed domain inquiry about a neural phenomenon, free of circularity, non‑trivial, and not reduced to an implementation constraint.
