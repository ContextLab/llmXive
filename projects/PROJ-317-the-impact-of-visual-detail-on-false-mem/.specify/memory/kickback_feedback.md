## Unresolved concerns

- The assumptions section (line ≈ 140) states the design is *observational* and that findings are *associational*, yet the hypothesis and success criteria claim a *causal* effect of visual detail on false‑memory susceptibility. This contradiction undermines causal validity; the plan must either adopt a purely experimental framing (random assignment to detail levels) or temper claims to association only.
- The power analysis relies on a “verified fact subject verify = 50” (Barnard’s test) without describing how within‑subjects correlation, sphericity, or the expected variance of false‑memory rates were incorporated. A proper a‑priori power calculation for repeated‑measures ANOVA should model the intra‑subject correlation (ρ) and the number of measurements per participant; otherwise the required N may be underestimated.
- Each participant is required to answer 20 true + 20 lure items per condition (≈ 120 trials total). The plan does not address potential fatigue or practice effects that could systematically inflate or deflate false‑memory rates across later conditions, introducing a confound not controlled by the Latin‑square order alone.
- Success Criterion SC‑001 demands a Cohen’s d ≥ 0.5, a large effect size that is not justified by the cited literature (Smith & Jones, 2020) which reports medium effects (d ≈ 0.3). Setting such a high threshold may render the study under‑powered or lead to false‑negative conclusions.
- The plan includes a primary Repeated‑Measures ANOVA (FR‑005 (primary)) which is not defined in the specification. The spec only requires a GLMM (FR‑005). This constitutes an over‑reach; the ANOVA task should be removed or linked to a new functional requirement.
- The functional requirements table lists FR‑005 twice (once for GLMM and once for Repeated‑Measures ANOVA) with the same identifier, which creates ambiguity when tracing requirements to implementation. Assign distinct IDs (e.g., FR‑005a for ANOVA and FR‑005b for GLMM) to maintain a one‑to‑one mapping.
- The plan references validation of responses against `contracts/response.schema.yaml` (FR‑011) but does not mention validation of stimulus metadata against the `contracts/stimulus.schema.yaml`. To ensure full contract coverage, add a step that validates each generated `metadata.yaml` file against the stimulus schema.
- The primary analysis uses a Repeated‑Measures ANOVA on false‑memory *rates* (proportions of “Yes” responses to lure items). Because each rate is derived from only 20 binary trials per condition, the normality and homoscedasticity assumptions of ANOVA are likely violated. In psychology, proportion data with limited trial counts are typically modeled with a binomial GLMM (or transformed with an arcsine‑square‑root) as the primary analysis. Relying on ANOVA as the main test risks inflated Type I error and undermines the validity of the claimed effect (SC‑001, SC‑002). The plan should either (a) make the GLMM the primary analysis and treat ANOVA as a descriptive supplement, or (b) apply an appropriate transformation and verify ANOVA assumptions before proceeding.
