**Author**: Implementation Team

## 1. Explicit Scope Statement

This study is strictly limited to measuring **behavioral false memory rates** in human participants in response to manipulated visual stimuli. The research design, data collection, and statistical analysis are confined to observable behavioral outputs (recognition accuracy, false alarm rates, response times).

**This study does NOT measure, infer, or claim to identify specific molecular, cellular, or synaptic mechanisms in humans.** Specifically, this project makes no claims regarding:

- CREB (cAMP response element-binding protein) activation levels
- Synaptic weight changes or long-term potentiation (LTP) in human neural tissue
- Specific protein kinase A (PKA) signaling pathways
- Molecular correlates of memory consolidation or reconsolidation
- Electrophysiological signatures of memory formation

## 2. Constitutional Constraint: Associational vs. Causal

This project operates under **Constitution VI**, which establishes a clear boundary between **associational** and **causal** claims in human subjects research.

### The "Associational vs. Causal" Constraint

Per Constitution VI, this study may only establish **statistical associations** between experimental variables (visual detail manipulation) and behavioral outcomes (false memory susceptibility). It explicitly **excludes** any task or analysis that attempts to map these behavioral outcomes to specific biological mechanisms in humans.

**Key Principle**: While biological mechanisms (e.g., synaptic plasticity) may *theoretically* underlie the observed behavioral phenomena, this project does not and cannot measure those mechanisms directly in human subjects. Any inference of mechanism would violate the ethical and methodological constraints defined in Constitution VI.

## 3. Theoretical Framework: Constructive Memory vs. Biological Mechanism

### 3.1 The Constructive Memory Framework

The theoretical foundation of this study is the **Constructive Memory** framework, which posits that memory is not a passive recording of events but an active, reconstructive process susceptible to distortion.

**Primary Citations**:

- **Loftus, E. F., & Palmer, J. C. (1974)**. "Reconstruction of automobile destruction: An example of the interaction between language and memory." *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 13(5), 585–589.
 - *Relevance*: Demonstrated that post-event information can alter memory representations, establishing the "misinformation effect" as a robust behavioral phenomenon.

- **Schacter, D. L. (1999)**. "The seven sins of memory: Insights from psychology and cognitive neuroscience." *American Psychologist*, 54(3), 182–203.
 - *Relevance*: Systematically categorized the constructive nature of memory errors (e.g., suggestibility, misattribution) as inherent features of human cognition rather than system failures.

- **Bartlett, F. C. (1932)**. *Remembering: A Study in Experimental and Social Psychology*. Cambridge University Press.
 - *Relevance*: Early foundational work showing that memory is reconstructed based on schemas and cultural expectations.

### 3.2 Contrast with Biological Mechanism Research

The biological mechanisms of memory formation have been extensively studied in non-human model organisms. Notably:

- **Kandel, E. R., et al. (various)**. Work on *Aplysia californica* demonstrated that sensitization and long-term memory involve specific molecular cascades (e.g., serotonin release, cAMP/PKA signaling, CREB-mediated gene expression, and synaptic growth).
 - *Reference*: Kandel, E. R. (2001). "The molecular biology of memory storage: a dialogue between genes and synapses." *Science*, 294(5544), 1030–1038.

**Critical Distinction**: While Kandel's work in *Aplysia* provides a mechanistic explanation for memory at the cellular level in a simple nervous system, **this does not translate to a direct measurement of synaptic changes in humans** for the following reasons:

1. **Methodological Limitation**: Human subjects research is ethically restricted to non-invasive behavioral, psychophysical, or neuroimaging (fMRI, EEG) measures. Direct measurement of synaptic proteins or molecular cascades is not feasible.
2. **Complexity Gap**: The human brain's complexity (86 billion neurons, trillions of synapses) precludes the direct mapping of specific molecular events to specific behavioral memories. [UNRESOLVED-CLAIM: c_9c0840c9 — status=not_enough_info]
3. **Construct Validity**: The behavioral phenomena studied here (false memory) are defined at the cognitive level. While they may be *supported* by biological processes, the study's constructs are behavioral, not molecular.

### 3.3 Integration: Why the Distinction Matters

This study adopts the **constructive memory framework** as its primary theoretical lens because:

- It provides a **testable, behavioral-level explanation** for the observed phenomena.
- It aligns with the **methodological constraints** of human subjects research.
- It avoids the **category error** of conflating behavioral outcomes with unmeasured biological mechanisms.

**Conclusion**: The biological work of Kandel and others inspires the hypothesis that memory is malleable, but this study does not claim to measure the biological implementation of that malleability in humans. The results will be interpreted as **associational evidence** for the constructive nature of memory, not as mechanistic proof of synaptic changes.

## 4. Implications for Analysis and Reporting

All statistical analyses and result interpretations in this project must adhere to the following language constraints:

- **Use**: "Association," "correlation," "behavioral correlate," "statistical relationship," "predictive relationship."
- **Avoid**: "Mechanism," "cause," "synaptic change," "molecular basis," "biological implementation," "neural encoding."

**Example**:
- *Correct*: "Increased visual detail is associated with a statistically significant reduction in false memory rates (p < 0.05)."
- *Incorrect*: "Increased visual detail causes a change in synaptic weights that reduces false memory."

- The project's explicit focus on behavioral constructs.

The project remains scientifically rigorous by establishing **behavioral associations** that are consistent with the constructive memory framework, while explicitly acknowledging the limits of inference regarding biological mechanisms.
