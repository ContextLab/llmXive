# Implementation Plan: Investigating the Relationship Between Brain Network Dynamics and Individual Differences in Musical Improvisation Skill

**Branch**: `001-gene-regulation` | **Date**: 2026-10-13 | **Spec**: [spec.md](../specs/001-gene-regulation/spec.md)  
**Input**: Feature specification from `/specs/001-gene-regulation/spec.md`

## Summary
The project will (1) acquire an open‑source fMRI‑derived time‑series dataset, (2) preprocess the data on a CPU‑only runner using Nilearn (≥ 0.10.0), (3) compute sliding‑window functional connectivity and graph‑theoretic metrics (global efficiency, modularity, participation coefficient) with Ledoit‑Wolf regularization, (4) reduce metric dimensionality with PCA, (5) test associations between the principal components and task condition (Improvisation vs Scripted) or years of training while controlling for age and head‑motion, (6) assess significance via 1 000 permutations followed by FDR correction, (7) conduct a sensitivity sweep over window/step sizes, and (8) produce NetworkX/Matplotlib visualizations. All steps are mapped to the functional (FR‑001 – FR‑008) and success‑criteria (SC‑001 – SC‑004) requirements.

## Phase & Task Mapping (covers all FR/SC)

| Phase | Tasks (brief) | FR IDs | SC IDs |
|-------|---------------|--------|--------|
| **0. Data Acquisition** | Download verified parquet OpenNeuro dataset; verify required columns (`subject_id`, `run_label`, `age`, `years_training`, `fd`, ROI time‑series). | FR‑001 | SC‑004 |
| **1. Preprocessing** | Use Nilearn to (a) motion‑correct, (b) slice‑time correct, (c) normalize to MNI, (d) band‑pass filter (0.01‑0.1 Hz); extract Schaefer‑100 ROI signals; output per‑subject CSV of shape (t × 100). | FR‑002 | SC‑004 |
| **2. Connectivity & Graph Metrics** | Sliding‑window Pearson correlation (window = 45 s, step = 10 s, min 30 TP); apply Ledoit‑Wolf shrinkage; compute global efficiency, modularity (Louvain), participation coefficient per window. | FR‑003 | — |
| **3. Dimensionality Reduction** | Stack per‑subject metric vectors; apply PCA (retain components explaining ≥ 80 % variance). | FR‑008 | — |
| **4. Primary Association Analysis** | Linear regression of PC scores on `task_condition` (binary) + covariates (`age`, `years_training`, `fd`); 1 000 permutation test; FDR correction across tested PCs. | FR‑004, FR‑005 | SC‑001, SC‑003 |
| **5. Sensitivity Analysis** | Repeat Phase 2‑4 for window sizes {30, 45, 60 s} and step sizes {5, 10 s}; record variation in regression coefficients; produce summary table. | FR‑006 | SC‑002 |
| **6. Visualization** | For high‑skill vs. low‑skill (or improv vs. scripted) groups, construct mean adjacency matrices; visualize differences with NetworkX (edge‑weight colormap) and Matplotlib. | FR‑007 | — |
| **7. Reporting & QC** | Log subjects excluded due to excess motion (FD > 0.5 mm > 20 % volumes); handle missing task labels (error‑halt or fallback to years‑of‑training). Produce `results/` folder with tables, figures, and a JSON summary. | — | SC‑001‑SC‑004 |

Each phase is ordered so that data is available before it is consumed, models are fitted before evaluation, and figures are generated before final reporting.

## Compute Feasibility
- All heavy lifting (Nilearn, scikit‑learn, NetworkX) runs comfortably on 2 CPU cores; memory usage stays < 6 GB by streaming subjects and using `numpy.memmap` for large windows.
- No GPU‑only libraries are required; the pipeline contains no `torch.cuda` calls, satisfying the CPU‑first policy.
