## Unresolved concerns

- The plan uses the 1982 ERA5 climate grid as a proxy for ambient temperature at the time of Moral Machine decisions (2014‑2018). This creates a construct‑validity problem: the temperature variable does not measure the actual exposure relevant to each decision, and spatial variation in the proxy may confound with cultural/latitude effects that are not fully controlled.
- Applying an Anderson‑Darling test for normality on deviance residuals of a mixed‑effects logistic regression is inappropriate—logistic models do not assume normally distributed residuals. This diagnostic requirement does not provide meaningful validation of model fit.
- The plan mentions using `statsmodels` `MixedLM` with a logit link, but `MixedLM` does not support GLM families. This creates a feasibility issue for the stated methodology and may lead to implementation failures or incorrect inference.
- Geographic matching is performed without explicitly controlling for latitude or regional climate gradients beyond the random intercept for `cultural_region`. Since temperature is highly collinear with latitude, residual confounding may remain, threatening internal validity.
- The indoor/outdoor confound is treated as optional and may be omitted if GHSL data is unavailable, leaving a known confound unaddressed without a predefined mitigation strategy.
- The plan asserts that the large sample size (>200 k after exclusions) provides ample power, but no a‑priori effect‑size estimate or power calculation is presented. Given the limited temperature variability (single‑year climate average), the effective variance of the predictor may be low, reducing detectable effect size.
- FR-003 requires a linear mixed‑effects model on log‑transformed response time (or a GLMM fallback). The plan replaces the outcome with a binary utilitarian choice and fits a mixed‑effects logistic regression, which does not satisfy the original functional requirement. The plan does not mark FR‑003 as scope‑limited or provide a justified deviation, leaving the requirement unaddressed.
- FR‑014 requires validation that all external sources meet the hourly resolution standard. The plan uses the ERA5 1982 HDF5 file, which provides only a long‑term climate average and lacks hourly data for the 2014‑2018 period of the Moral Machine records. The plan merely logs a warning instead of rejecting the source, violating the constitution’s Verified Accuracy principle.
- FR‑001 mandates merging Moral Machine responses with hourly ERA5 temperature data using geographic coordinates **and timestamps** as keys. Because the chosen ERA5 file contains only a single‑year 1982 snapshot, temporal matching cannot be performed. The plan marks this as “partial” and proceeds with a climate‑proxy merge, which does not satisfy the functional requirement as written.
- Constitution Principle II / FR‑014 requires that any external source that does not meet the hourly‑resolution standard be **rejected** before ingestion. The plan’s ‘Validate Sources’ step only logs a warning and proceeds with the 1982 ERA5 snapshot, which violates the Verified Accuracy principle.
- The plan never references the `contracts/moral_temperature.schema.yaml` contract, leaving that schema unused. Every contract must be exercised by at least one pipeline step.
- The plan mentions generation of `data/processed/data_quality_log.json` but does not explicitly state that this file conforms to the `exclusion_log.schema.yaml` contract; an explicit reference would close the plan‑to‑contract traceability gap.
- The analysis uses the 1982 ERA5 mean‑temperature grid as a proxy for ambient temperature experienced by participants in 2014‑2018 (see the ‘Scope Adjustment’ paragraph). This temporal mismatch means the predictor does not reflect the actual temperature at decision time, undermining the validity of any claim about “ambient temperature influence”. The methodology should either obtain a temporally aligned temperature source or explicitly re‑frame the research question to address long‑term climatic differences only.
- The plan applies an Anderson‑Darling test for normality on deviance residuals of a mixed‑effects logistic regression (SC‑005). Logistic models do not assume normally distributed residuals; testing for normality is inappropriate and may lead to misleading diagnostics. Replace this with appropriate checks (e.g., Hosmer‑Lemeshow goodness‑of‑fit, calibration plots, or examination of Pearson/standardized residuals).
