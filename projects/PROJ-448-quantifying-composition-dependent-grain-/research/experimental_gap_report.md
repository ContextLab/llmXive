## Executive Summary

This report documents the absence of verified experimental Atom Probe Tomography (APT) data for **ternary** BCC Fe alloy systems (Fe-Cr-Mo, Fe-Cr-V, Fe-Mo-V, Fe-Cr-W, Fe-Mo-W) required to satisfy Success Criterion SC-003. While the computational pipeline successfully models segregation profiles using surrogate DFT energies and the McLean isotherm, the lack of direct experimental measurement for multicomponent systems prevents quantitative validation of the cooperative effect hypothesis.

## Findings

### Binary Systems (Fe-Cr, Fe-Mo, etc.)
Experimental data exists for several binary subsystems (e.g., Fe-Cr), as documented in `research/data_sources.md`. These data points were used to calibrate the surrogate models and validate the thermodynamic consistency of the pipeline (Task T095c).

### Ternary Systems (Fe-Cr-Mo, etc.)
**No verified experimental APT datasets were found** for the target ternary systems.
* **Fe-Cr-Mo**: No public APT dataset containing grain boundary segregation profiles for this specific ternary combination was located.
* **Fe-Cr-V / Fe-Mo-V / Fe-Cr-W / Fe-Mo-W**: Similarly, no raw APT data or published concentration profiles matching the specific boundary conditions (Σ5 tilt, BCC) were identified.

## Implications for SC-003

Success Criterion SC-003 requires: *"Validate computed segregation energies against experimental literature values."*

Due to the absence of ternary data:
1. **Binary Validation**: The pipeline satisfies SC-003 partially for binary systems where data exists.
2. **Ternary Validation**: SC-003 **cannot be fully satisfied** for the ternary systems. The "cooperative effects" (non-linear interactions between solutes) predicted by the regression model (Task T025) remain **unverified by direct measurement**.

The current validation is limited to:
* Thermodynamic consistency checks against CALPHAD (Task T017c).
* Statistical significance of interaction terms (Task T023).
* Cross-validation stability (Task T030).

These constitute *internal* validation of the model logic, but not *external* experimental verification of the physical phenomenon in multicomponent alloys.

## Recommended Path Forward

To bridge this gap and achieve full SC-003 compliance:

1. **Experimental Campaign**: Initiate an APT study targeting Fe-Cr-Mo and Fe-Cr-V alloys.
 * **Specimen Preparation**: FIB lift-out of Σ5 tilt grain boundaries.
 * **Measurement**: Laser-pulsed APT at cryogenic temperatures (50K) to minimize thermal evaporation artifacts.
 * **Target**: 10-20 GB boundary regions per specimen to ensure statistical significance of segregation peaks.
2. **Data Acquisition**: Collaborate with national labs (e.g., NIST, LBNL) to access existing unpublished datasets.
3. **Model Refinement**: Once ternary data is obtained, re-run the regression analysis (Task T021b) to update interaction coefficients and validate the "cooperative effect" hypothesis quantitatively.

## Conclusion

The computational pipeline is robust and internally consistent. However, the specific scientific claim regarding **composition-dependent cooperative segregation in ternary BCC Fe alloys** lacks the necessary experimental ground truth for final validation. This report formally acknowledges the gap and defines the experimental requirements to close it.
