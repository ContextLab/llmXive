## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass

The question asks about a fundamental physical relationship: how the spatial embedding of nodes in a network modifies macroscopic epidemic dynamics (threshold and peak rate). It compares a physical reality (geographic constraints) against a theoretical abstraction (topology-only), which is a substantive scientific inquiry independent of the specific simulation code used to measure it.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor variable is the empirical geographic distance between nodes (derived from external coordinate data or geocoding), while the predicted variable is the resulting epidemic trajectory (threshold and peak infection) generated by the SIR simulation. These are independent data sources; the geographic coordinates are not derived from the infection dynamics, nor is the infection dynamics a direct mathematical summary of the coordinates without the stochastic simulation step.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass

A positive result (geography increases the threshold) confirms that physical distance acts as a barrier, refining existing spatial epidemic theories for real-world networks. A null result (geography has no effect on the threshold in scale-free topologies) would be highly informative, potentially revealing that the "small-world" property of scale-free networks dominates over spatial constraints in certain regimes. Neither outcome is predetermined by basic domain knowledge.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass

The question explicitly names a domain relationship: the alteration of epidemic dynamics by geographic proximity. It does not frame the inquiry around the performance of a specific algorithm, the speed of a specific hardware setup, or the ability of a specific library to handle a dataset, but rather focuses on the emergent behavior of the system under different structural assumptions.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated

All four checks pass, indicating a robust, scientifically meaningful research question that correctly isolates a physical phenomenon from methodological constraints. The project is ready to advance to project initialization.
