## Unresolved concerns

- The plan states ‘Run **10** Monte‑Carlo replicates per configuration (FR‑003)’. Ten replicates are far too few to obtain stable estimates of epidemic thresholds or to power the two‑sample permutation tests, especially given the stochastic nature of SIR dynamics on scale‑free graphs. This undermines the claimed ≥ 80 % power and risks high variance in effect‑size estimates.
- Synthetic‑control coordinates are generated by sampling uniformly in the unit square. Real airports are highly clustered (e.g., around population centers). A uniform control therefore conflates spatial heterogeneity with the effect of proximity, providing a weak null that may exaggerate the observed spatial effect.
- The epidemic threshold is defined as the smallest β₀ where ≥5 % of nodes become infected in >50 % of MC runs. This 5 % infection criterion is arbitrary and may not correspond to a true phase transition in a scale‑free network, leading to biased threshold estimates.
- If the graph exceeds ~10 k nodes the plan ‘uniformly down‑samples to 10 k’. Uniform node down‑sampling can distort the heavy‑tailed degree distribution that underpins the scale‑free property, potentially altering epidemic dynamics and invalidating comparisons across runs.
- The design does not explicitly control for possible correlation between node degree and geographic location (e.g., hubs may be geographically clustered). Without accounting for this confound, observed differences between empirical and randomized coordinates could be driven by degree‑location coupling rather than pure spatial proximity.
- Phase 7 is listed without any associated FR or SC. Contract validation is already covered by FR‑016 in Phase 6, so this entry appears redundant and constitutes an over‑reach beyond the specification.
- The plan states ‘run **10** Monte‑Carlo replicates per configuration (FR‑003)’ but the Constitution (Principle VII) mandates **50 runs per configuration**. This violates the computational boundedness requirement and will cause a requirement‑level mismatch.
- The β₀ sweep is listed as ‘{0.01, 0.06, 0.11, 0.16, 0.21, 0.26, 0.31, 0.36, 0.41, 0.46}’, omitting the upper bound 0.51 required by FR‑013 (sweep [0.01, 0.51] in 0.05 steps).
- The plan claims that 10 Monte‑Carlo runs per configuration provide ≥ 80 % power to detect an effect size ≥ 0.1 at α = 0.05 (see FR‑003). With only 10 samples per group the statistical power of a two‑sample permutation test (or any t‑like test) is far lower than 80 % for such a small effect; this claim is unsupported and makes the planned significance testing under‑powered.
