## Unresolved concerns

- The research question asks about the impact of *network centrality* on neural synchrony, yet the implementation plan uses *inter‑hemispheric coherence* as the predictor (see Summary lines 1‑3). Coherence is a functional‑connectivity metric, not a graph‑theoretic centrality measure, so the construct being measured does not match the construct named in the spec. This threatens construct validity and makes the claim about “network centrality” unsupportable.
- The plan states that Bonferroni correction will be applied across **30 planned tests** (see Computational Feasibility section), while the research document lists only **8 primary tests** (2 bands × 4 sleep stages). This inconsistency leaves the multiple‑comparison correction undefined, which directly affects the alpha level used and the subsequent power analysis.
- A fixed minimum sample size of **N ≥ 30** subjects is enforced (see Statistical Rigor). After Bonferroni correction (α = 0.05/8 ≈ 0.00625 or possibly 0.05/30), a sample of 30 provides very low power to detect medium‑sized effects (r≈0.3). No a‑priori power calculation is performed, so the study is likely under‑powered, and the post‑hoc power analysis cannot rescue the design.
- Covariate adjustment is performed via partial correlation, but the plan does not specify how missing covariate data will be handled beyond a flag. If covariates are missing for many subjects, the effective sample size for adjusted analyses may drop substantially, further reducing power and potentially introducing bias.
- Spec FR-007 requires Benjamini‑Hochberg FDR correction, but the plan implements Bonferroni correction (see FR/SC Coverage Matrix row for FR‑007). This diverges from the functional requirement and needs either the spec to be updated or the plan to implement BH as specified.
- Spec FR-010 mandates a minimum subject count of N ≥ 50 before analysis, whereas the plan validates N ≥ 30 (see FR‑010 description in the matrix). The lower threshold does not satisfy the functional requirement.
- The plan cites the Sleep‑EDF dataset (via a Hugging Face mirror) as the source for analysis, but the research question explicitly targets *network centrality* metrics. With only two EEG channels (Fpz‑Cz, Pz‑Oz) the dataset cannot support meaningful graph‑theoretic centrality calculations (degree, betweenness, eigenvector, etc.). Instead, the plan computes inter‑hemispheric coherence as a predictor, which does not address the stated centrality hypothesis. This mismatch makes the chosen dataset inappropriate for the declared research question.
- Plan states Bonferroni correction across 30 tests, but the analysis contract (contracts/analysis.schema.yaml) defines p_value_corrected as raw p × 8 (capped at 1.0), implying 8 tests. This mismatch violates the contract and creates ambiguity in multiple‑comparison control.
- Research document describes 8 primary correlation tests (2 coherence metrics × 4 sleep stages), while the plan’s FR‑007 entry claims 30 tests. The differing test counts conflict and break consistency across design artifacts.
- Schema hard‑codes Bonferroni factor of 8, but plan’s FR‑007 uses a factor of 30. The plan must align with the contract or the contract must be updated; otherwise the generated analysis_results.json will not validate.
- The plan computes *inter‑hemispheric coherence* (theta and alpha bands) as the sole waking‑state predictor (see FR‑004) but the research question explicitly asks whether **network centrality** metrics predict sleep‑stage synchrony. Coherence is not a graph‑theoretic centrality measure (e.g., degree, eigenvector, betweenness) and therefore the analysis does not actually test the stated impact of *network centrality*. This constitutes a fundamental mismatch between the research question and the operationalized metric, rendering the planned validation scientifically invalid.
