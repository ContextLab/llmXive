## Unresolved concerns

- The plan enforces a minimum sample size of N ≥ 30 (FR‑010) but also applies a Bonferroni correction across 30 hypothesis tests, yielding a per‑test α ≈ 0.0017. With only 30 subjects this combination yields very low statistical power for medium‑sized effects, making it unlikely to detect any true association.
- Centrality metrics are computed from a waking‑state functional‑connectivity graph that includes EEG, EOG, and EMG channels. Mixing neural (EEG) and non‑neural (EOG/EMG) signals may compromise construct validity of the ‘network centrality’ predictor for brain‑based synchrony.
- The underlying assumption that waking‑state centrality is stable and predictive of sleep‑stage synchrony is not empirically justified; no validation of centrality stability across states is planned, raising concerns about the validity of the causal pathway implied by the research question.
- Potential confounds beyond age, gender, and total sleep time (e.g., medication, sleep quality, EEG noise levels) are not addressed, which could bias the observed associations.
- The plan references a Shapiro‑Wilk normality test (FR‑012) in `analysis.py` and in Phase 3 tasks, but the specification (`spec.md`) does not define FR‑012. This introduces a functional requirement that is not present in the spec, constituting an over‑reach.
- The plan cites the Sleep‑EDF dataset (via a Hugging Face mirror) as the source for ‘waking‑state functional connectivity matrices’. However, Sleep‑EDF recordings consist of only two EEG derivations (Fpz‑Cz, Pz‑Oz) plus auxiliary EOG/EMG channels, and the waking segment is limited to a brief pre‑sleep period (often < 2 min). Using such short, non‑resting wake recordings to estimate reliable brain‑wide functional connectivity—and then computing degree, betweenness, and eigenvector centrality on a network that includes peripheral channels (EOG, EMG) – is questionable for the research question, which seeks meaningful centrality variation across subjects. This constitutes a mismatch between the dataset’s content and the analytical goals (wrong‑dataset‑for‑the‑question).
- Centrality metrics are computed on a graph that includes all recorded channels (EEG Fpz‑Cz, Pz‑Oz, EOG, EMG). EOG and EMG are peripheral, non‑cortical signals; incorporating them into a functional‑connectivity network intended to reflect brain network topology conflates neural and non‑neural activity. This undermines the interpretability of degree, betweenness, and eigenvector centrality as *neural* predictors of sleep‑stage PLV and may introduce systematic bias. Consider restricting the network to EEG channels only, or at least reporting separate centrality analyses with and without peripheral channels.
