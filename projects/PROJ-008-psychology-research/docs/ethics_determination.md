# Ethics Determination: Secondary Analysis of Public Registry Data

**Project**: PROJ-008-psychology-research
**Study Title**: Mindfulness Components and Delivery Formats in ASD Social Skills
**Date**: 2024-05-15
**Status**: **EXEMPT** (No IRB Approval Required)

## 1. Determination Summary

This research project involves a **systematic review and meta-analysis** of de-identified, publicly available data from clinical trial registries (ClinicalTrials.gov and OSF). The study does not involve direct interaction with human subjects, collection of private identifiable information, or intervention.

Per the **Common Rule (45 CFR 46)** and standard institutional policies, this work qualifies as **Exempt Category 4**: "Secondary research for which consent is not required: Secondary research uses of identifiable private information or identifiable biospecimens, if the information is recorded by the investigator in such a manner that the identity of the human subjects cannot readily be ascertained directly or through identifiers linked to the subjects."

Specifically, the data sources are:
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**: Publicly accessible registry of federally and privately supported clinical trials.
- **Open Science Framework (OSF)**: Public repository for research materials and pre-registered studies.

All data extracted are summary statistics, intervention descriptions, and study metadata that are already in the public domain. No individual participant data (IPD) is accessed, and no re-identification efforts are undertaken.

## 2. Justification for Exemption

### 2.1 Nature of Data
The data used in this analysis consists entirely of:
- Study titles and registration numbers
- Publication status and abstracts
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria (aggregate descriptions)
- Intervention descriptions (mindfulness components, delivery formats)
- Outcome measures (standardized scale names)
- Aggregate effect sizes and sample sizes (Hedges' *g*, *N*, *SE*)

**No personally identifiable information (PII)** such as names, dates of birth, addresses, or medical record numbers is collected or processed.

### 2.2 Source of Data
The data sources (ClinicalTrials.gov and OSF) are **public registries**. Access to this data does not require credentials that would imply a private relationship between the researcher and the subject. The data is already disclosed to the public for the purpose of transparency in clinical research.

### 2.3 Risk to Subjects
Because the data is de-identified and publicly available, the risk of harm to subjects is **no more than minimal**. There is no possibility of breach of confidentiality that could lead to social, legal, or economic harm.

## 3. Regulatory Basis

This determination aligns with:
- **45 CFR 46.104(d)(4)**: Exemption for secondary research using publicly available information.
- **FDA 21 CFR 56.104(c)**: Exemption for research involving the use of existing data/specimens that are publicly available or recorded in such a way that subjects cannot be identified.

## 4. Data Integrity and Privacy Safeguards

Although IRB approval is not required, the project adheres to the following safeguards to ensure ethical data handling:
- **Strict Source Limitation**: Data is sourced *only* from ClinicalTrials.gov and OSF as mandated by Constitution Principle VI.
- **No Re-identification**: The research team will not attempt to link registry data to other datasets to identify individuals.
- **Data Anonymity**: All analysis is performed on aggregate data. No individual-level records are stored.
- **Transparent Reporting**: All methods and data sources are documented in `docs/protocol.md` to ensure reproducibility and transparency.

## 5. Conclusion

Based on the secondary analysis of de-identified public registry data, this project is **Exempt** from full IRB review. No formal IRB submission or approval number is required for the conduct of this research.
