# Research Specification: Mindfulness Interventions for ASD Social Skills

## Research Question
What is the efficacy of mindfulness-based interventions for improving social skills
in children aged 6-12 with Autism Spectrum Disorder, and how do efficacy estimates
vary by mindfulness component (breathing, body scan, mindful movement) and delivery
format (individual, group, parent-mediated)?

## Hypotheses

### Primary Hypothesis
Mindfulness-based interventions will show a moderate positive effect size (Hedges' g ≥ 0.5)
on social skill outcomes compared to control conditions.

### Secondary Hypotheses
1. Interventions including multiple mindfulness components will show larger effects
 than single-component interventions.
2. Group delivery formats will show larger effects than individual formats.
3. Parent-mediated interventions will show sustained effects at follow-up (≥3 months).

## Novel Contribution
This meta-analysis addresses a gap in the literature by:
- Focusing specifically on the 6-12 age range (often under-represented in existing reviews)
- Disaggregating mindfulness components to identify active ingredients
- Analyzing delivery formats to inform implementation decisions
- Providing the first systematic synthesis of delivery format efficacy in this population

## Data Sources
- **ClinicalTrials.gov**: Primary registry for RCTs
- **Open Science Framework (OSF)**: Supplementary registry for pre-registered studies

## Inclusion Criteria
- Population: Children aged 6-12 with ASD diagnosis
- Intervention: Mindfulness-based (breathing, body scan, mindful movement)
- Comparator: Waitlist, treatment-as-usual, or active control
- Outcome: Validated social skill measure (e.g., SRS, ABC, VABS)
- Design: RCT or quasi-experimental with control group

## Exclusion Criteria
- Age outside 6-12 range
- No ASD diagnosis or unspecified diagnosis
- Non-mindfulness intervention (e.g., CBT only, ABA only)
- No control group or single-arm design
- Non-social skill outcome as primary endpoint
- Full-text not available (abstract-only extraction only)

## Missing Data Handling
- Primary: Multiple imputation (if ≤20% missing)
- Secondary: Complete-case analysis
- Sensitivity: Worst-case/best-case scenarios for missing outcomes

## Heterogeneity Assessment
- I² statistic: >50% indicates substantial heterogeneity
- Cochran's Q: p < 0.10 indicates significant heterogeneity
- Subgroup analysis: Mindfulness components, delivery format, social skill domain

## Publication Bias Assessment
- Funnel plot (only if N ≥ 10)
- Egger's test (only if N ≥ 10)
- Trim-and-fill method if asymmetry detected

## Limitations
- Potential for small-study effects
- Heterogeneity in outcome measures
- Limited number of studies may constrain subgroup analyses
- Abstract-only extraction may miss key methodological details
