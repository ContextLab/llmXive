## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass

The question investigates the fundamental relationship between training dynamics (logits, gradient norms) and the physical reality of hardware quantization effects on policy divergence. It is not asking whether a specific algorithm performs well, but rather whether specific training signals theoretically contain the information necessary to bound a physical phenomenon (quantization error) across different hardware implementations.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor features (logits and gradient norms) are derived strictly from the full-precision training state, while the predicted variable (policy gap divergence) is measured by comparing full-precision outputs against actual hardware quantized outputs. These are independent data sources; the ground truth is generated by the physical constraints of the inference engine, not by a simulation or a mathematical transformation of the training features themselves.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass

A positive result would be highly significant, proving that expensive real-time hardware synchronization can be replaced by cheap analytical bounds, fundamentally changing how LLM RL is scaled. A null result would be equally informative, demonstrating that training signals fail to capture the non-linearities of hardware quantization, thereby proving that synchronous hardware checks remain a necessary bottleneck for stability.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass

The question names a substantive domain relationship (the information-theoretic link between training gradients and quantization-induced policy drift) rather than a constraint on a specific implementation method. While it mentions "hardware inference engines," this is the object of study (the source of truth) rather than a constraint on the research method (e.g., "Can we do this on a CPU in 6 hours?").

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated

All checks pass; the research question addresses a genuine gap between theoretical training bounds and physical hardware realities without falling into circularity or implementation-narrowing traps. The distinction between the training signal and the hardware-measured ground truth is clear, and the potential outcomes (successful proxy vs. inherent limitation) are both scientifically valuable.
