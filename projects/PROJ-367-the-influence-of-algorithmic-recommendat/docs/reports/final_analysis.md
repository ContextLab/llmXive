# Final Analysis Report: The Influence of Algorithmic Recommendations on Exploration vs. Exploitation

## Executive Summary

This report presents an associational analysis of the relationship between algorithmic recommendation diversity and learner enrollment diversity. The study utilizes Propensity Score Weighting (PSW) to adjust for observed confounders and employs robustness checks via residual permutation tests.

## Methodology

### Data Ingestion and Diversity Metrics
Data was ingested from `data/raw/synthetic_enrollments.csv` (generated per T014b). We computed Shannon entropy (log base 2) for both `recommended_categories` and `enrolled_categories` to derive `Recommendation_Diversity_Score` and `Learner_Diversity_Score`.

### Modeling Approach
To isolate the influence of the algorithm, we derived a `Baseline_Interest_Vector` from pre-study history. We applied Propensity Score Weighting (PSW) to balance covariates and fit a weighted linear regression. In cases where the sample size was insufficient (N < 30) or weight stability checks failed, a Generalized Least Squares (GLS) fallback with robust standard errors was utilized.

### Robustness Verification
A residual permutation test (1,000 iterations) was conducted to assess the stability of the observed coefficient against a null distribution. ## Limitations and Methodological Assumptions

### The Baseline_Interest_Vector Assumption
**Critical Assumption**: This study relies on the `Baseline_Interest_Vector` as the **sole proxy** for intrinsic user preferences. As per the project specification (spec.md Assumptions), we assume this vector adequately captures the user's pre-existing interest state prior to algorithmic intervention.

**Implication of Missing Utility Function**:
Without access to an explicit utility function or the user's internal reward model (payoff structure), the observed correlation `Recommendation_Diversity -> Learner_Diversity` is consistent with two distinct theoretical interpretations:
1. **Passive Manipulation (Algorithmic Influence)**: The algorithm actively shapes user behavior towards more diverse content.
2. **Active Equilibrium Strategy**: Users with diverse intrinsic preferences (captured imperfectly by the baseline vector) select into diverse recommendations, and the algorithm is merely reflecting these pre-existing preferences.

Consequently, the "Algorithmic Influence" metric derived in this study must be framed strictly as a **predictor of variance** in learner diversity, rather than a definitive causal effect size. The study cannot distinguish between passive manipulation and active equilibrium strategy due to the missing utility function.

### Observational Nature
Findings are strictly associational. No causal claims are made due to the lack of randomization in the data generation process. The results represent a sensitivity metric for unmeasured confounding rather than a causal effect.

## Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates a robust association between algorithmic recommendation diversity and learner exploration behaviors, stable across semantic similarity thresholds. However, due to the methodological limitation of the missing utility function, these findings should be interpreted as evidence of correlation and predictive capacity, not as proof of causal manipulation. Future work incorporating explicit utility modeling or randomized trials is required to disentangle algorithmic influence from user equilibrium strategies.
