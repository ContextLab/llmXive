## Unresolved concerns

- The plan uses a stratified split by `pairing_id`. If each `pairing_id` is unique per experiment, this stratification provides no protection against data leakage; material‑level information can still appear in both train and test sets, confounding model evaluation.
- Hyper‑parameter tuning is performed with a single k‑fold CV on the training data, then the best model is evaluated on the held‑out test set. Without a nested‑CV or separate validation split, the CV results may be optimistically biased, threatening the validity of the reported R² ≥ 0.7 target.
- Roughness descriptors are computed with `pySurf` only if raw profilometry files are present; otherwise the plan falls back to existing columns without verifying that those columns are comparable to the `pySurf` outputs. This creates a construct‑validity risk: the predictor variables may not consistently represent the intended physical measures.
- The power analysis triggers a switch to a non‑parametric test when power < 0.8 or n < 200, but the plan does not specify the assumed effect size, variance, or alpha used to compute power. Consequently, the sample‑size justification for the R² ≥ 0.7 target is missing, risking under‑powered conclusions.
- The plan treats the power threshold (0.8) as a gating condition for test choice, yet the same threshold is also used as a success criterion (SC‑005) for runtime. Mixing statistical power considerations with unrelated performance metrics can lead to ambiguous decision rules.
- The plan cites OpenML dataset ID 42123 as the primary source for surface‑roughness and tribology measurements, but no verified URL or citation is provided and the OpenML catalogue does not contain a dataset with those exact columns (Sa, Sq, Ssk, Sku, Sp, Sv, coeff_friction, wear_rate). Using a dataset that lacks the required features would make the entire pipeline unable to meet the functional requirements (FR‑001, FR‑012) and the success criteria (SC‑001/SC‑002). Provide a verified, publicly reachable dataset that explicitly includes the needed roughness descriptors and target variables, or adjust the requirements to match the actual content of the OpenML dataset.
- The Constitution I (Single Source of Truth) is marked ✅ in the Constitution Check table, but the plan does not explicitly designate which artifact (e.g., a specific data file, schema, or results JSON) serves as the project's SSoT. Constitution Principle I requires an explicit SSoT designation to ensure traceability of all figures and statistics.
- The plan stratifies the train‑test split by `pairing_id`. In the data model each `pairing_id` uniquely identifies a single experiment, so stratification provides no effective grouping and may lead to data leakage if the same material pair appears only once. This undermines the intended robustness across material pairings.
- Outlier removal is performed using a ±3σ rule, which assumes the underlying feature distributions are approximately Gaussian. Surface‑roughness descriptors (e.g., Sa, Sq) are often positively skewed, making the σ‑based rule inappropriate and potentially discarding valid extreme values.
- Derived fields `avg_hardness`, `avg_modulus`, and `hardness_modulus_ratio_avg` are linear combinations of the original hardness and modulus features, yet the plan treats them as independent predictors alongside the originals. This introduces multicollinearity, violating the independence assumption of many regression models and inflating variance of coefficient estimates.
