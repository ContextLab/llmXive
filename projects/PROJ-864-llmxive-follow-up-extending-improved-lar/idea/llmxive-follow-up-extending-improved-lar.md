# llmXive follow-up: extending "Improved Large Language Diffusion Models"

**Field**: Computational Linguistics / Machine Learning

## Research question

How does the overfitting trajectory of bidirectional diffusion language models during pre-training on limited data compare to autoregressive baselines, and does the "overfitting-as-a-feature" phenomenon observed in supervised fine-tuning generalize to the pre-training regime?

## Motivation

While the original iLLaDA work demonstrates that bidirectional diffusion models can outperform autoregressive baselines in SFT settings through extensive data repetition, it remains unknown if this "overfitting-as-a-feature" phenomenon scales to pre-training on limited, high-quality data. Verifying this on a constrained dataset would determine if high-performance non-autoregressive models can be trained on standard CPU clusters without the 12T-token compute budgets currently required, democratizing access to efficient language model training.

## Related work

- [Improving Variable-Length Generation in Diffusion Language Models via Length Regularization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07546) — Establishes that diffusion models rely on a fixed-length canvas, a fundamental architectural constraint that may impact learning dynamics on small corpora compared to autoregressive models.
- [DiffusionBERT: Improving Generative Masked Language Models with Diffusion Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15029) — Demonstrates that discrete diffusion models share training objectives with pre-trained language models, providing a baseline for comparing pre-training behaviors on smaller scales.
- [Discrete Diffusion Modeling by Estimating the Ratios of the Data Distribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16834) — Highlights the specific challenges standard diffusion models face on discrete data domains like natural language, which motivates the comparison with autoregressive baselines in this study.

## Expected results

We expect the bidirectional diffusion model to exhibit a slower degradation in validation performance (perplexity) compared to the autoregressive model as training epochs increase beyond the point of initial convergence. Confirmation of the hypothesis would be a statistically significant divergence where the diffusion model's validation loss plateaus at a lower level while the autoregressive model's loss rises, indicating superior resistance to overfitting on the limited Micro-Corpus.

## Methodology sketch

- **Data Curation**: Construct a "Micro-Corpus" of exactly 10 million tokens by filtering open-source datasets (e.g., subsets of Project Gutenberg or The Stack) to ensure a balanced mix of logical and code-heavy text, suitable for CPU training.
- **Model Architecture**: Implement two 100M-parameter models: (1) a standard causal autoregressive transformer and (2) a bidirectional masked diffusion model, ensuring identical embedding dimensions and attention heads to isolate architectural differences.
- **Training Protocol**: Execute a repeated-epoch training loop for 100 epochs on the Micro-Corpus for both models using a CPU-optimized loop (e.g., `torch.compile` on CPU) with identical batch sizes and learning rate schedules.
- **Evaluation Schedule**: Record validation loss (perplexity) and accuracy on a held-out test set (1M tokens) after every epoch to explicitly map the overfitting trajectory of both architectures.
- **Independent Validation**: Evaluate final checkpoint performance on an **independent** benchmark suite (e.g., a subset of BigBench or HumanEval) that was **not** used in the training corpus or the Micro-Corpus construction, ensuring the validation metric is not a function of the training data distribution.
- **Statistical Analysis**: Perform a repeated-measures ANOVA on the validation loss curves across epochs to test for a significant interaction between model type and epoch count, specifically checking if the diffusion model's performance degrades significantly slower.
- **Resource Monitoring**: Log CPU RAM usage and wall-clock time per epoch to verify feasibility within the 7GB RAM / 6-hour job constraints.
