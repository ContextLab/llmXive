# Feature Specification: Constraining Lorentz Invariance Violation with Fermi Gamma‑Ray Burst Spectra

**Feature Branch**: `001-constrain-liv-fermi`  
**Created**: 2026-06-16  
**Status**: Draft  
**Input**: User description: "Constraining Lorentz Invariance Violation with Fermi Gamma‑Ray Burst Spectra"

## User Scenarios & Testing

### User Story 1 - Retrieve and Pre-process Brightest Fermi-GBM Bursts (Priority: P1)

The researcher MUST be able to download light-curve data for the top brightest GRBs from the Fermi Science Support Center, re-bin them into three specific energy bands (low, medium, and high), and subtract background counts to generate a clean dataset for lag analysis.

**Why this priority**: This is the foundational data ingestion step; without clean, correctly binned time-series data, no spectral lag can be measured, and the entire study fails.

**Independent Test**: The system can be tested by running the ingestion script on a small subset of known GRBs and verifying that the output CSV contains time bins with photon counts for exactly three energy bands and that background-subtracted counts are non-negative.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the HEASARC Fermi GBM Trigger Catalog is accessible, **When** the script queries for the top 30 bursts by peak flux (50-300 keV) using the API endpoint `https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/FTP/fermi/data/gbm/triggers/`, **Then** the script downloads light curves for exactly 30 events and saves them to a local `data/raw` directory.
2. **Given** a raw light curve file exists, **When** the re-binning function is applied, **Then** the output contains three distinct columns for the specified energy bands with consistent time alignment.
3. **Given** a background region is defined in the metadata, **When** background subtraction is performed, **Then** the system clips any negative counts to 0. If the clipped count in a bin exceeds 30% of the total signal in that bin, the burst is excluded from the global average to prevent bias > 5%. (See FR-009).

### User Story 2 - Compute Spectral Lags and Estimate LIV Bounds (Priority: P2)

The researcher MUST be able to compute the cross-correlation function (CCF) between the low-energy band and high-energy bands to identify the spectral lag, estimate its uncertainty via bootstrap resampling, and perform a population-based Hierarchical Bayesian Regression (including redshift as a covariate and intrinsic lag as a random intercept) to derive an upper bound on the linear LIV energy scale ($E_{\rm QG}$) for each burst and the population.

**Why this priority**: This implements the core physics methodology; it transforms raw data into the specific scientific metric ($E_{\rm QG}$) required to answer the research question while accounting for intrinsic source lags as a nuisance parameter.

**Independent Test**: The system can be tested by feeding synthetic light curves with a known, injected time lag (e.g., 50 ms) into the pipeline; the output must recover a lag within the 95% confidence interval of the injected value. The injected value is generated by injecting a known lag into a synthetic light curve where the intrinsic lag is modeled as a random intercept from a Normal distribution.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** background-subtracted light curves for a single GRB, **When** the CCF is computed between the 8–50 keV and 200–1000 keV bands, **Then** the lag is identified as the time offset at the CCF peak position.
2. **Given** a measured lag and its bootstrap-derived uncertainty, **When** the Hierarchical Bayesian Regression against photon energy is performed using the cosmological distance $D$ and redshift $z$ as covariates with a random intercept for intrinsic lag (modeled as a nuisance parameter with a Normal prior), **Then** the system outputs a 95% confidence upper limit for $E_{\rm QG}$ in units of GeV, matching the injected synthetic value within 5%.
3. **Given** multiple bursts have been processed, **When** the global limit is calculated via Hierarchical Bayesian Meta-Analysis (using PyMC), **Then** the system combines individual burst estimates to produce a single aggregate upper bound, accounting for non-Gaussian errors.

---

### User Story 3 - Validate Robustness via Null Distribution and Sensitivity Analysis (Priority: P3)

The researcher MUST be able to generate a null distribution of the global $E_{\rm QG}$ slope estimate by shuffling the measured spectral lags against the redshift/energy covariates [deferred] times (preserving the distribution of intrinsic lags but breaking the LIV energy-dependence), and sweep the energy-band definitions (e.g., varying boundaries by ±10%) to verify that the final limit is stable.

**Why this priority**: This ensures the result is scientifically defensible, distinguishing true physical signals from statistical noise or systematic biases, which is a mandatory requirement for publication-quality physics results.

**Independent Test**: The system can be tested by running the Monte Carlo simulation on the full population; the resulting null distribution of $E_{\rm QG}$ estimates must be centered near zero (or the mean intrinsic lag) and show no systematic bias (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test p-value > 0.05 against a zero-centered distribution).

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the observed population of lags, **When** 10,000 synthetic populations are generated by shuffling the (lag, redshift, energy) tuples across the entire population (preserving the marginal distribution of lags but breaking the correlation with energy), **Then** the pipeline computes a null distribution of $E_{\rm QG}$ estimates that shows no systematic bias toward low energy scales (KS test p-value > 0.05).
2. **Given** the primary energy bands (8–50 keV and 200–1000 keV), **When** the boundaries are shifted by ±10% (e.g., 8–45 keV vs 8–55 keV), **Then** the derived $E_{\rm QG}$ limit varies by less than 20%, confirming robustness to binning choices.
3. **Given** the final aggregated limit, **When** compared against the null distribution, **Then** the system flags the result as "consistent with null" if the observed limit falls within the 95% quantile of the simulated background.

### Edge Cases

- What happens when a GRB has insufficient photon counts in the highest energy band to compute a statistically significant CCF peak? (System must flag the burst as "excluded" and exclude it from the global weighted average).
- How does the system handle GRBs without known redshift values? (The pipeline must strictly exclude these from the $E_{\rm QG}$ calculation, as the cosmological distance $D$ is a mandatory input for the linear dispersion relation; no imputation method is applied to avoid introducing unquantified systematic errors > 10%. (See FR-009)).
- How does the system handle negative counts after background subtraction? (Clipped to zero; bursts with >30% clipped counts are excluded per FR-009).

## Requirements

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: System MUST download light-curve files for the top 30 brightest GRBs from the HEASARC Fermi GBM Trigger Catalog using the API endpoint `https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/FTP/fermi/data/gbm/triggers/` with the query parameter `peak_flux>1e-7` (Peak Flux in 50-300 keV) and sort by `peak_flux` descending.
- **FR-002**: System MUST re-bin light curves into three fixed energy bands (8–50 keV, 50–200 keV, 200–1000 keV) and perform background subtraction.
- **FR-003**: System MUST compute the cross-correlation function (CCF) between the lowest and highest energy bands to identify the spectral lag peak.
- **FR-004**: System MUST estimate the statistical uncertainty of the spectral lag using bootstrap resampling of photon arrival times (≥ 1,000 resamples).
- **FR-005**: System MUST perform a Hierarchical Bayesian Regression of lag vs. photon energy, including redshift as a covariate and a random intercept for intrinsic source lag (modeled as a nuisance parameter with a Normal prior), to derive a 95% confidence upper bound on $E_{\rm QG}$.
- **FR-006**: System MUST combine individual burst $E_{\rm QG}$ estimates using a Hierarchical Bayesian Meta-Analysis (implemented via PyMC) with weakly informative priors (Normal(0, 10) for log-scale) to produce a global upper bound, accounting for non-Gaussian errors and systematic biases.
- **FR-007**: System MUST generate a null distribution of the global $E_{\rm QG}$ slope estimate by shuffling the measured spectral lags against the redshift and energy covariates [deferred] times. This method preserves the marginal distribution of intrinsic lags while breaking the energy-dependent LIV correlation, ensuring the null hypothesis represents "Lag = Intrinsic Lag" (LIV=0).
- **FR-008**: System MUST perform a sensitivity analysis by varying energy-band boundaries by ±10% and reporting the variation in the final $E_{\rm QG}$ limit.
- **FR-009**: System MUST exclude a burst from the global analysis if: (a) it lacks a known redshift, OR (b) the clipped negative count in any bin exceeds a substantial fraction of the total signal in that bin (based on Fermi GBM analysis standards for background validity).
- **FR-010**: System MUST perform a sensitivity analysis on the 30% clipping threshold by sweeping values in {[deferred], [deferred], [deferred], [deferred]} and reporting the variation in the global $E_{\rm QG}$ limit.
- **FR-011**: System MUST validate the regression model by analyzing a control sample of low-redshift bursts (z < 0.1) to test for correlation between intrinsic lag and redshift.

### Key Entities

- **GRBEvent**: Represents a single Gamma-Ray Burst, including attributes: `grb_id`, `redshift`, `sky_position`, `fluence_rank`, `peak_flux`.
- **LightCurve**: Represents the time-series data for a specific energy band, including attributes: `time_bins`, `photon_counts`, `background_counts`.
- **SpectralLag**: Represents the measured time delay between energy bands, including attributes: `lag_ms`, `uncertainty_ms`, `ccf_peak_position`.
- **LIVConstraint**: Represents the derived physical limit, including attributes: `energy_scale_gev`, `confidence_level`, `burst_id`.

## Success Criteria

### Measurable Outcomes

> Planning docs state *what* will be measured and the *source/reference* it is measured against; defer specific empirical values (counts, dataset sizes, measured quantities, percentages) to the implementation/research phase.

- **SC-001**: The 95% confidence upper limit on the linear LIV energy scale ($E_{\rm QG}$) is measured against the literature benchmark of $\sim10^{19}$ GeV to determine if the result is competitive.
- **SC-002**: The consistency of the derived limit across the sample of bright GRBs is measured against the variance of individual burst estimates to ensure no single burst contributes > 40% to the global weight.
- **SC-003**: The absence of systematic bias is measured by comparing the observed $E_{\rm QG}$ distribution against the null distribution generated from 10,000 simulated datasets where lags are shuffled against redshift/energy covariates.
- **SC-004**: The robustness of the result is measured by the percentage change in the final $E_{\rm QG}$ limit when energy-band boundaries are swept by ±10%; the change must be < 20%.

## Assumptions

- The HEASARC Fermi GBM Trigger Catalog provides public access to the required light-curve files for the top 30 brightest GRBs without authentication barriers.
- Redshift values are available for at least 80% of the top 30 brightest GRBs; bursts without redshift will be excluded from the $E_{\rm QG}$ calculation.
- The linear dispersion relation $\Delta t = \frac{E}{E_{\rm QG}} \frac{D}{c}$ is the correct model for the LIV effect at the energy scales probed by Fermi-GBM.
- The free-tier GitHub Actions runner (multi-core CPU, ~7 GB RAM) is sufficient to process 30 GRBs and run a statistically robust set of Monte Carlo simulations within the 6-hour job limit., provided the data is sampled or processed in batches.
- The background subtraction method used in the standard GBM offline tools is applicable to the re-binned data without requiring custom algorithmic adjustments.
- The spectral lag is dominated by intrinsic source effects or LIV, and instrumental time delays are negligible or corrected for in the raw data.
- The PyMC library is available and configured for Hierarchical Bayesian Meta-Analysis.
- All four schema files (`grb_event.schema.yaml`, `light_curve.schema.yaml`, `spectral_lag.schema.yaml`, `liv_constraint.schema.yaml`) are generated in Phase 1 to align the plan with the data model.
