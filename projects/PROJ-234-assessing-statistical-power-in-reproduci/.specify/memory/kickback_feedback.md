## Unresolved concerns

- The plan uses a fixed MDES threshold (MDES ≤ 0.5) as a proxy for ‘adequate power’ (see line 5 of the Summary). This threshold is introduced without theoretical justification or citation, and MDES is an effect‑size metric, not a power metric; using it to judge adequacy conflates constructs and may lead to misleading conclusions.
- MDES is computed exclusively with `statsmodels.stats.power.tt_solve_power` (t‑test) or `FTestAnovaPower` (ANOVA) for all studies (step 5). Many classification studies use logistic regression, non‑parametric tests, or other designs; applying a t/ANOVA model to those datasets violates the assumption that the chosen statistical test matches the original analysis, threatening construct validity of the MDES estimates.
- Extraction of sample size (N) and effect size relies on “robust regex/NLP” (step 4) with only a spot‑check of 5 papers for validation. This limited validation does not quantify extraction error rates across the full corpus, and no systematic sensitivity analysis is described beyond a binary “full‑text vs abstract” flag, leaving the measurement process under‑controlled.
- The dataset selection strategy (top‑50 most‑downloaded OpenML classification datasets) introduces a clear sampling bias toward popular studies, yet the plan does not include any strategy to account for this confound (e.g., weighting, stratification, or discussion of its impact on generalizability). Without such control, the audit results may not be representative of the broader literature.
- Spec FR‑004 requires calculation of **observed statistical power** for each dataset, but the plan only describes computing Minimum Detectable Effect Size (MDES) in script `03_compute_mdes.py`. No script or step addresses observed power, leaving FR‑004 unbacked.
- Spec FR‑005 calls for a report containing a **histogram of power values** and a count of studies with power < 0.8. The plan’s report (`04_generate_report.py`) instead produces a histogram of MDES and counts MDES ≤ 0.5, which does not satisfy FR‑005.
- Spec SC‑002 measures the proportion of studies with **power < 0.8**. The plan’s success criterion (SC‑002) measures the proportion with **MDES ≤ 0.5**, which is a different metric and does not fulfill the specified success criterion.
- The plan does not explicitly designate a Single Source of Truth (SSoT) file as required by Constitution Principle I. While it mentions checksum‑driven hygiene and that figures reference rows in `data/processed/`, it never names a concrete artifact (e.g., `data/metadata.json`) as the authoritative SSoT for the project.
- The data model defines an `ExtractionAuditReport` entity, but the implementation plan never mentions generating or validating such a report. This creates a mismatch where an entity is defined but unused.
- The plan treats the computed Minimum Detectable Effect Size (MDES) as a proxy for ‘adequate power’ by counting studies with MDES ≤ 0.5 (see step 5 and SC‑002). MDES is a deterministic function of sample size, α and target power; it is not a measure of the study’s actual power nor of whether the study was sufficiently powered for its observed effect. Using an arbitrary MDES threshold (0.5) to declare a study ‘adequately powered’ is not justified in the statistical literature and conflates two distinct concepts (design‑level detectability vs. achieved power). This constitutes a scientifically unsound claim and makes the primary evaluation circular: the outcome (MDES ≤ 0.5) is directly derived from the predictor (sample size) without independent validation.
