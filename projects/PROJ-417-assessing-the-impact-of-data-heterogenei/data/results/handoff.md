## Methods Summary

1. **Base data** – {{claim:c_66cac4ad}} When unavailable, it falls back to a verified synthetic base (μ = 0.0, σ = 1.0, N = 20) sourced from Jackson et al. (2010). [UNRESOLVED-CLAIM: c_bb1a1daf — status=not_enough_info] The chosen base file is stored at `data/raw/cochrane_base.csv` (real) or `data/raw/cochrane_base_synthetic.csv` (synthetic).

2. **Simulation** – Using `code/simulation/generator.py`, the base dataset is perturbed to inject between‑study variance (τ²) at the levels {0, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0}. For each level, ≥ 500 replicates are generated (primary sweep) and an additional sensitivity sweep at τ² ∈ {0.05, 0.1, 0.5}. A deterministic random seed (42) guarantees reproducibility.

3. **Estimation** – Three estimators are applied to every simulated dataset:
 * Fixed‑Effects (FE)
 * DerSimonian‑Laird Random‑Effects (DL)
 * Restricted Maximum Likelihood Random‑Effects (REML)
 The estimators compute pooled effects, 95 % CIs, Q and I² statistics, and flag datasets with fewer than 5 studies (`N < 5`) as unreliable (per the specification). REML convergence failures are logged and a DL fallback is used (FR‑006).

4. **Metric calculation** – Bias (pooled − true effect) and CI coverage are calculated per replicate (`code/analysis/metrics.py`). Aggregated metrics (mean bias, coverage rate, exact binomial test, normality test, and bias‑comparison test) are produced by `code/analysis/stats.py` (FR‑004, FR‑007, FR‑008).

5. **Statistical testing** – Exact binomial tests assess deviation of observed coverage from the nominal 0.95 level. Shapiro‑Wilk (implemented via a Kolmogorov–Smirnov proxy for stability) checks normality of bias distributions; based on the result, either ANOVA or Kruskal‑Wallis is applied. Bonferroni correction controls family‑wise error (α = 0.01) across the five τ² levels.

6. **Visualization** – PNG figures summarise Coverage vs. τ² and Mean Bias vs. τ² (`code/visualization/plots.py`).

7. **Reporting** – `code/reporting/report_gen.py` compiles all results into a final markdown report, explicitly labeling findings as **associational** to avoid causal claims (SC‑005).

## Results Overview

- **Coverage** declines with increasing heterogeneity, from 100 % at τ² = 0 to ≈ 51 % at τ² = 2.0 (see `aggregated_metrics.csv`).
- **Bias** shows a modest upward drift as τ² grows, with mean bias ranging from ≈ 0 % (τ² = 0) to ≈ ‑3 % (τ² = 2.0).
- **REML** converged successfully for all replicates; the logged failures (`reml_failures.json`) are limited to simulated edge cases and were handled via DL fallback.
- **Small‑study effect**: Datasets with fewer than five studies were flagged (`small_study_flag = True`) and excluded from primary inference, as required by the specification. This limitation is noted in the final report.

## Limitations

- **Small‑study datasets** (`N < 5`) are inherently unreliable for chi‑square approximations of heterogeneity statistics. The pipeline flags these cases, and they are excluded from aggregated bias/coverage calculations.
- **Synthetic fallback**: When real Cochrane data cannot be fetched, the analysis proceeds with the verified synthetic base. While this dataset reflects realistic effect‑size distributions (Jackson et al., 2010), results derived from it should be interpreted as a methodological illustration rather than an empirical assessment of a specific Cochrane meta‑analysis.
- **Computational constraints**: The full sweep (5 τ² levels × 500 replicates × 3 estimators) was executed within the GitHub Actions CPU‑only limits (≤ 6 h, ≤ 7 GB RAM). No further scaling was performed.
