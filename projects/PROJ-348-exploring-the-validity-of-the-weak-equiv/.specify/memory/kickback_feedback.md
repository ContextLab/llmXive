## Unresolved concerns

- The plan proposes fitting PPN parameters ($\gamma, eta, ta$) directly to 'raw range/range-rate tracking data' using a single N-body integration baseline. This approach lacks the necessary degrees of freedom to separate the Nordtvedt effect (which depends on planetary binding energy $\Omega$) from the dominant PPN $eta$ and $\gamma$ terms. Without a differential analysis (comparing residuals of planets with different $\Omega$) or a full ephemeris fit that simultaneously solves for all parameters and initial conditions, the correlation between residuals and $\Omega$ will be confounded by the much larger secular precession terms common to all planets. The proposed 'secondary regression' on residuals is circular if the baseline already assumes GR (where $ta=0$).
- The Monte Carlo strategy is flawed for the stated goal. The plan states: 'We will NOT re-integrate the N-body system 10,000 times... Instead, we will fit the linear regression model on the residuals.' This tests the statistical significance of the *linear regression slope* on a fixed set of residuals, but it does not test the validity of the *dynamical model* or the parameter estimation of $ta$. To validate the constraint on $ta$ or $\omega_{BD}$, the MC must propagate uncertainties through the *dynamical model* (or at least the full parameter estimation step), not just the final linear regression of precession rates. The current approach conflates the uncertainty of the regression slope with the uncertainty of the physical parameter $ta$.
- The plan explicitly rejects the Constitution's requirement for `scipy.integrate.odeint` in favor of `rebound` IAS15. While `rebound` is scientifically superior for N-body dynamics, the Constitution (FR-030, Principle VII) mandates `scipy.integrate.odeint` to 'isolate secular precession trends'. This is a direct conflict. If the plan proceeds with `rebound`, it violates the Constitution's specific integrator constraint. If it switches to `scipy.odeint` for a 75-year, 4-body integration with GR corrections, it risks numerical instability and drift that could mimic or mask the Nordtvedt signal, invalidating the causal claim of the results.
- The plan assumes a linear relationship `residual ~ \Omega` for the Nordtvedt effect. However, the Nordtvedt effect manifests as a polarization of the orbit (a specific time-dependent perturbation), not just a simple shift in the secular precession rate. Fitting a static precession rate residual against $\Omega$ ignores the time-domain signature of the effect. This construct validity issue means the measurement (precession rate residual) does not accurately capture the construct (Nordtvedt polarization), leading to a high probability of false negatives or incorrect bounds.

- The plan proposes a 'Secondary Regression' where the dependent variable is 'Residual precession rate (Observed - GR Baseline)' and the independent variable is 'Gravitational binding energy fraction ($\Omega$)'. This is a circular validation. The 'Observed' data comes from JPL Horizons, which is an ephemeris that *already incorporates* General Relativity and the Nordtvedt effect (if any) in its solution. The 'GR Baseline' is a numerical integration of the *same* GR physics. Subtracting them yields residuals dominated by measurement noise and unmodeled non-gravitational forces, not a clean signal of $ta$. Furthermore, $\Omega$ is a static property of the planet, while the 'residual precession rate' is derived from the *same* ephemeris that defines the planet's orbit. If the ephemeris assumes GR ($ta=0$), the residuals will be zero (within noise) regardless of the true $\Omega$, making the regression tautological (testing if noise correlates with a constant). The Nordtvedt effect is a *difference* in acceleration between bodies with different $\Omega$; it cannot be detected by regressing the *residuals of a single ephemeris* against $\Omega$ unless the ephemeris was explicitly generated with a *wrong* $ta$ and the residuals are compared to a *correct* one. The plan lacks a mechanism to generate 'synthetic' data with a known non-zero $ta$ to test against, or to use the *difference* between two independent ephemerides (JPL vs INPOP) as the dependent variable, which is the only valid way to isolate the effect without circularity.
- The plan states the integrator will be validated by matching 'the theoretical GR value (~43 arcseconds/century)'. This is a tautology. The `rebound` integrator with the Schwarzschild term *implements* the GR equations of motion. If the code is correct, it *must* produce ~43 arcsec/century. Validating the code against the theory it implements does not validate the *physics* of the solar system or the presence of WEP violations. It only validates the numerical implementation. The scientific question is whether the *observed* precession matches the *predicted* precession. The plan conflates 'code validation' (does the integrator work?) with 'physics validation' (does the solar system obey GR?). The latter requires comparing the integrator's output to *independent* observational data (e.g., radar ranging residuals), not to the theoretical value itself.
- The plan proposes fitting PPN parameters ($\gamma, eta, ta$) directly to 'raw range/range-rate tracking data' using a simple non-linear least squares fit on a 4-body system. This is methodologically unsound for the claimed precision. Modern ephemerides (JPL, INPOP) use thousands of data points, complex force models (tides, solar radiation pressure, asteroid perturbations), and sophisticated estimation techniques (batch least squares with covariance propagation) to achieve meter-level accuracy. A simple 4-body fit with only Schwarzschild correction will have residuals orders of magnitude larger than the Nordtvedt signal (which is ~10s of meters for Earth-Moon, but planetary effects are subtle). The 'residuals' from such a crude model will be dominated by unmodeled physics (e.g., asteroid belt mass, solar oblateness $J_2$), completely swamping the $ta$ signal. The plan fails to account for the necessity of a full force model and the specific data types (e.g., LLR for Earth-Moon, not just planetary range) required to constrain $ta$.
- The plan claims 'Keplerian-only models cannot account for the ~43 arcsec/century precession... to isolate the Nordtvedt effect'. This is true, but the proposed solution (N-body + Schwarzschild) is insufficient. The Nordtvedt effect is a *violation* of the Strong Equivalence Principle, manifesting as a polarization of the Earth-Moon orbit towards the Sun. It is primarily constrained by Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR), not planetary orbital precession. While planetary orbits are affected, the signal is extremely small and degenerate with other parameters (like $J_2$ of the Sun). The plan focuses on Mercury/Venus/Earth/Mars precession, which is the wrong observable for a robust test of the Nordtvedt parameter $ta$ using public data. The primary constraint on $ta$ comes from the Earth-Moon system, not the heliocentric orbits of the inner planets. The plan's choice of observable (planetary precession) is scientifically weak for the specific parameter $ta$.
