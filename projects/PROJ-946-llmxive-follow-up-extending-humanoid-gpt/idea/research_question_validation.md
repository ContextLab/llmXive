## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: fail

The question is framed around a specific methodological constraint ("non-differentiable controllers," "decision trees," "k-NN") rather than a fundamental scientific phenomenon about humanoid motion. It asks whether a specific class of simple algorithms fails to capture dynamics, which is an engineering benchmark result rather than a discovery about the nature of motion itself. The underlying phenomenon question would be: "What is the minimum representational complexity required to model the dynamics of complex human motion?"

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor inputs are raw kinematic states (joint angles, velocities) derived from the motion capture data, while the target variable is the joint trajectory generated by the Teacher Transformer. These are distinct data processing paths: one is the raw observation, the other is a high-level model output. There is no mechanical guarantee that the simple models will fail or succeed based solely on data construction.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: concern

There is a significant risk that the outcome is predetermined by domain knowledge: it is widely accepted in control theory and machine learning that non-differentiable, shallow models (like decision stumps) cannot approximate the continuous, high-dimensional manifolds required for complex dynamic control. A null result (simple models fail) would likely be viewed as a confirmation of existing theory rather than a novel insight, while a positive result (simple models succeed) would be surprising but might be dismissed as an artifact of the specific dataset or teacher quality rather than a generalizable rule.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: fail

The question explicitly names implementation constraints (CPU-only, non-differentiable, specific algorithm types like k-NN) as the core of the inquiry. It frames the research as a test of whether "simple logic" can replace "complex Transformers," which is a comparative implementation question. A valid domain question would focus on the *properties of the motion data* itself (e.g., "Do the temporal dependencies in complex human motion require continuous latent representations?") without pre-judging the algorithmic mechanism.
