# Symmetry Group Definition for Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS)

## 1. Formal Definition of Group G

In the context of Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution (EAS), the reaction site is defined by the aromatic ring system. The physical process of electrophilic attack involves the interaction of an external electrophile ($E^+$) with the $\pi$-electron cloud of the ring.

Let $G_{ring}$ be the molecular graph of the aromatic reactant (e.g., benzene, toluene, nitrobenzene). The vertices $V$ represent atoms, and edges $E$ represent bonds.

We define the **Symmetry Group $G$** for EAS as the subgroup of the full graph automorphism group $\text{Aut}(G_{ring})$ that preserves:
1. The aromatic nature of the ring (preserving the cycle structure).
2. The set of atoms susceptible to electrophilic attack (the $sp^2$ hybridized carbons in the ring).
3. The relative orientation of substituents already present on the ring (distinguishing ortho, meta, and para positions relative to existing groups).

Mathematically, $G$ is the group of permutations $\sigma \in S_n$ (where $n = |V|$) such that:
- $\sigma$ maps the set of ring atoms to itself.
- $\sigma$ preserves adjacency: $(u, v) \in E \iff (\sigma(u), \sigma(v)) \in E$.
- $\sigma$ preserves atom types (e.g., Carbon vs. Heteroatom) and bond orders (aromatic vs. single/double).
- $\sigma$ fixes the "attack site" orbit, meaning it maps the set of equivalent reaction sites to itself.

For a mono-substituted benzene $C_6H_5-R$, the group $G$ is isomorphic to the point group $C_{2v}$ (or a subgroup thereof depending on the symmetry of $R$), acting on the ring carbons. The group elements correspond to the identity, a reflection across the plane containing the substituent $R$ and the para-carbon, and potentially a $180^\circ$ rotation if $R$ is symmetric.

## 2. List of Generators

The group $G$ is generated by a minimal set of transformations that, when composed, produce all valid symmetry operations for the specific reactant topology.

### Case A: Benzene ($C_6H_6$)
For unsubstituted benzene, the full symmetry group of the ring is the Dihedral group $D_6$. However, in the context of EAS where we distinguish the "attack site" relative to a hypothetical reference, we often consider the stabilizer of a specific carbon if we are analyzing a specific reaction coordinate.

If we consider the reactant *before* attack (isolated molecule), the relevant symmetry group for invariance of topological indices is the full automorphism group of the graph:
- **Generator 1 ($r$)**: Rotation by $60^\circ$ (cyclic permutation of ring atoms $1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4 \to 5 \to 6 \to 1$).
- **Generator 2 ($s$)**: Reflection across the axis passing through atom 1 and atom 4 (swaps $2 \leftrightarrow 6$, $3 \leftrightarrow 5$).

### Case B: Mono-substituted Benzene ($C_6H_5-R$)
Assuming $R$ is not symmetric with respect to the ring plane (e.g., $-CH_3$, $-NO_2$), the symmetry is reduced. The group $G$ is isomorphic to $C_{2v}$ (or $C_s$ if $R$ breaks the $C_2$ axis).

- **Generator 1 ($\sigma_v$)**: Reflection across the plane containing the substituent $R$ and the para-carbon.
 - Permutation: Fixes the substituent-bearing carbon ($C_1$) and the para-carbon ($C_4$). Swaps ortho pairs ($C_2 \leftrightarrow C_6$) and meta pairs ($C_3 \leftrightarrow C_5$).

If the substituent $R$ itself possesses a symmetry axis aligned with the ring (e.g., $-C(CH_3)_3$), the $C_2$ rotation might be preserved, adding a generator. However, for general topological analysis, we assume the minimal symmetry $C_s$ generated by the reflection $\sigma_v$.

### Case C: Global vs. Local Permutations
- **Global Graph Automorphisms**: These are permutations of the entire graph $G_{ring} \cup \text{substituents}$. They must preserve the connectivity of the entire molecule.
- **Local Site Permutations**: These are permutations restricted to the ring atoms that preserve the connectivity to the substituent. The group $G$ defined above is the intersection of the global automorphism group and the set of permutations that map the set of ring carbons to itself.

## 3. Proof of Invariance for Wiener Index

The **Wiener Index** $W$ of a molecular graph is defined as the sum of the shortest path distances between all pairs of vertices:
$$ W = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u \in V} \sum_{v \in V} d(u, v) $$
where $d(u, v)$ is the graph distance (number of edges in the shortest path) between vertices $u$ and $v$.

### Theorem
Let $G$ be the symmetry group defined in Section 1. For any $\sigma \in G$, the Wiener index of the graph is invariant:
$$ W(G) = W(\sigma(G)) $$

### Proof
1. **Definition of Automorphism**: By definition, an automorphism $\sigma$ of a graph $G=(V, E)$ is a bijection $\sigma: V \to V$ such that for any $u, v \in V$:
 $$ (u, v) \in E \iff (\sigma(u), \sigma(v)) \in E $$

2. **Distance Preservation**: Since adjacency is preserved, the existence of a path of length $k$ between $u$ and $v$ implies the existence of a path of length $k$ between $\sigma(u)$ and $\sigma(v)$.
 - If $P = (u, v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{k-1}, v)$ is a path in $G$, then $\sigma(P) = (\sigma(u), \sigma(v_1), \dots, \sigma(v))$ is a path in $G$.
 - Therefore, $d(\sigma(u), \sigma(v)) = d(u, v)$.
 - Since $\sigma$ is a bijection, the shortest path in the image corresponds exactly to the shortest path in the pre-image.

3. **Invariance of the Sum**:
 Substitute the distance property into the Wiener index formula for the permuted graph:
 $$ W(\sigma(G)) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{x \in V} \sum_{y \in V} d(x, y) $$
 Let $x = \sigma(u)$ and $y = \sigma(v)$. As $u$ and $v$ range over all $V$, so do $x$ and $y$ (since $\sigma$ is a bijection).
 $$ W(\sigma(G)) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u \in V} \sum_{v \in V} d(\sigma(u), \sigma(v)) $$
 Since $d(\sigma(u), \sigma(v)) = d(u, v)$:
 $$ W(\sigma(G)) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u \in V} \sum_{v \in V} d(u, v) = W(G) $$

### Conclusion
The Wiener index is a topological invariant under any graph automorphism. Since the group $G$ defined for EAS is a subgroup of the full automorphism group, the Wiener index is strictly invariant under the transformations in $G$.

This proof holds similarly for other topological indices that depend solely on graph distances (e.g., Balaban index, Zagreb index) or connectivity, provided they are defined as functions of the adjacency matrix or distance matrix which are invariant under permutation similarity transformations $P A P^T$.

## 4. Distinction: Global vs. Local Permutations

- **Global Automorphisms**: These consider the entire molecular graph, including substituents. If a substituent $R$ is asymmetric (e.g., $-CH_2Cl$), the only global automorphism might be the identity.
- **Local Site Permutations**: In the context of EAS, we are often interested in the symmetry of the *ring* relative to the *substituent*. The group $G$ defined here allows us to identify equivalent positions (e.g., ortho positions 2 and 6 are equivalent because there exists a $\sigma \in G$ such that $\sigma(2)=6$).

The invariance of the Wiener index ensures that calculating the index on the "canonical" representation of the molecule yields the same value regardless of how the atoms are numbered, as long as the numbering corresponds to a valid automorphism. This is critical for the reproducibility of the descriptor calculations in the `code/descriptors.py` module.
