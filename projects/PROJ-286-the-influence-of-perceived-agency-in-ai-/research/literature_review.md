# Literature Review Summary

**Topic**: The Influence of Perceived Agency in AI Interactions on Trust

## Key Findings

### Lee & See (2004) - Trust in Automation

Lee and See (2004) conducted a seminal study on trust in automation, establishing a comprehensive framework for understanding how humans develop and maintain trust in automated systems. Their research identified 12 key dimensions of trust, which were operationalized into a 12-item scale. [UNRESOLVED-CLAIM: c_ced9fb1a — status=not_enough_info] Key findings include:

- Trust is a multifaceted construct involving beliefs about the system's competence, predictability, and benevolence.
- Perceived control and agency significantly influence trust levels. When users feel they have control over the system's actions, trust tends to be higher.
- The 12-item scale developed by Lee and See has been widely validated and is a standard measure in human-automation interaction research.

### Langer (1975) - The Illusion of Control

Langer's (1975) classic study on the "illusion of control" demonstrated that people often overestimate their ability to control outcomes, even in situations where outcomes are entirely chance-determined. Key insights relevant to this study include:

- When individuals are given the opportunity to make choices or exert control, they perceive the outcome as more favorable, even if the actual outcome is unchanged.
- This illusion of control can lead to increased confidence and trust in systems, even when the system's actual performance remains constant.
- The psychological mechanism of perceived agency can be leveraged to enhance user trust in AI systems, provided that the illusion does not lead to harmful over-reliance.

## Implications for Current Study

The current study aims to investigate how perceived agency (manipulated through High vs. Low agency conditions) affects trust in AI recommendations. Based on Lee & See (2004), we expect that higher perceived agency will correlate with higher trust scores. Drawing from Langer (1975), we hypothesize that even illusory control (where the AI's output is unchanged) may significantly boost perceived trust, highlighting the psychological power of agency perception.

These theoretical foundations support the experimental design, which manipulates perceived agency while keeping the actual AI behavior constant, to isolate the effect of perception on trust.
