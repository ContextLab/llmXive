## Limitations

### Methodological Constraints
- **Fixation Threshold Sensitivity**: While we conducted robustness analyses across multiple thresholds, the choice of fixation detection parameters may still influence results.
- **ROI Definition**: The predefined ROIs may not capture all relevant areas of visual attention (e.g., comments, images).
- **Cross-Sectional Design**: Our analysis cannot establish causal relationships; longitudinal or experimental designs are needed.

### Data Limitations
- **Dataset Specificity**: Results may be specific to the Dundee Eye-Tracking Corpus and may not generalize to other contexts.
- **Sample Size**: The number of participants and headlines may limit the precision of random effect estimates.
- **Lexicon Coverage**: The fallback to VADER when NRC coverage is low introduces potential heterogeneity in valence measurement.

### Measurement Issues
- **Self-Reported Belief**: Belief ratings are subjective and may not reflect actual belief change.
- **Ecological Validity**: Laboratory eye-tracking may not fully capture real-world information processing.

## Future Directions

### Extended Methodologies
- **Eye-Tracking with EEG**: Combining eye-tracking with neural measures could provide insights into the timing of belief formation.
- **Naturalistic Settings**: Mobile eye-tracking in real-world contexts could improve ecological validity.
- **Longitudinal Designs**: Tracking belief changes over time could establish causal relationships.

### Additional Variables
- **Source Credibility**: Explicit measures of perceived source credibility could clarify the role of source attribution.
- **Emotional Arousal**: Physiological measures (e.g., skin conductance) could complement valence ratings.
- **Social Context**: The influence of social cues (e.g., likes, shares) on attention and belief.

### Intervention Studies
- **Pre-bunking**: Testing whether exposure to misleading headlines followed by corrective information reduces susceptibility.
- **Attention Training**: Interventions to encourage attention to source attribution before reading headlines.
- **Cognitive Reflection Training**: Programs to improve critical thinking skills and reduce misinformation susceptibility.

## Conclusion
This study provides empirical evidence for the complex interplay between visual attention patterns, headline valence, and cognitive reflection in predicting belief ratings of potentially misleading headlines. The findings highlight the importance of considering multiple factors simultaneously when investigating misinformation susceptibility. Future research should build on these findings to develop more effective interventions and to further elucidate the cognitive mechanisms underlying belief formation in the digital age.
