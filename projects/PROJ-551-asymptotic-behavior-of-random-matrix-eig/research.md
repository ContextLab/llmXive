# Research: Asymptotic Behavior of Random Matrix Eigenvalues with Sparse Perturbations

## 1. Introduction

This project investigates the asymptotic behavior of eigenvalues of large random matrices (Wigner matrices) perturbed by sparse, low-rank matrices. The primary focus is the identification of the Baik-Ben Arous-Péché (BBP) phase transition, where eigenvalues detach from the bulk spectrum (the semicircle law support) when the perturbation strength exceeds a critical threshold $\theta_c$.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 The Computational Observer

In this study, the "observer" is defined strictly as the deterministic computational algorithm (the spectral solver) that measures statistical correlations within the simulated data ensemble. The observer does not interact with a physical system; it operates on mathematical constructs generated by code.

The probability distributions derived (e.g., the probability of an outlier emerging at a given $\theta$) are properties of the algorithm's ensemble of random matrix instances, not of a physical stochastic process. The "frame of reference" is the coordinate system of the matrix indices and the spectral axis, defined by the numerical precision of the solver.

### 2.2 Mathematical Model

The core model consists of:
1. **Wigner Matrix ($W_N$)**: An $N \times N$ symmetric matrix with independent, identically distributed entries (scaled by $1/\sqrt{N}$) satisfying the semicircle law.
2. **Sparse Perturbation ($P_N$)**: A deterministic or random sparse matrix of rank $k$, characterized by a strength parameter $\theta$ and a support density.
3. **Perturbed Matrix ($M_N = W_N + P_N$)**: The object of spectral analysis.

### 2.3 Simulation Protocol

- **Generation**: Matrices are generated using `numpy` and `scipy` with explicit seed control for reproducibility.
- **Spectral Analysis**: Eigenvalues are computed using iterative solvers (`scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh`) to handle large $N$ efficiently within CPU memory constraints.
- **Threshold Detection**: A parameter sweep over $\theta$ is performed to empirically determine the critical value $\theta_c$ where outliers emerge.
- **Sensitivity Analysis**: The robustness of $\theta_c$ is tested against variations in sparsity density.

## 3. Results

(Results are detailed in the generated reports and data files within `data/processed/` and `data/figures/`.)

- **Phase Transition**: The simulation confirms the theoretical prediction that outliers emerge when $\theta > 1$ (for the standard scaling used).
- **Threshold Identification**: The critical threshold $\theta_c$ is identified with high precision via logistic regression on the outlier detection data.
- **Sensitivity**: The threshold remains stable across varying support densities, confirming the robustness of the BBP transition in this ensemble.

## 4. Limitations and Scope

This study is purely observational and computational. It verifies the BBP transition within a defined mathematical ensemble.

- **Mathematical Constructs**: The "sparse perturbations" used in this study are algorithmic parameters (sparse matrices with specific rank and density). They are not physical noise, thermal fluctuations, or environmental decoherence.
- **The Observer**: The "observer" is the solver code measuring the spectrum. No physical observer, measurement apparatus, or frame of reference in the sense of relativity or quantum mechanics is modeled or claimed to be modeled.
- **No Physical Claim**: This project does not claim to model any specific physical system (e.g., quantum dots, neural networks, or financial markets). The findings are strictly about the spectral properties of random matrix ensembles with added structure. Any analogy to physical systems is illustrative, not a claim of physical reality.

## 5. Conclusion

The computational experiments successfully reproduce the BBP phase transition. The critical threshold $\theta_c$ is identified and shown to be robust against variations in the sparsity pattern of the perturbation. The study remains strictly within the bounds of mathematical simulation, with the "observer" defined as the algorithmic process of spectral measurement.
