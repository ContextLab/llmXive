## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: concern

The question asks whether the *geometric structure* of intermediate steps drives performance compared to *high-dimensional learned embeddings*, which is a substantive scientific question about the nature of spatial reasoning representations. However, the phrasing "minimal representational complexity required" risks conflating the theoretical capacity of the representation with the specific implementation constraints (CPU-only, <10M parameters) mentioned in the methodology. While the core inquiry is about representation types (symbolic vs. learned), the current framing leans slightly toward an engineering benchmark of "what is the smallest model that works" rather than a pure investigation of the representational mechanism itself.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor (symbolic coordinate vectors or learned embeddings) is an intermediate representation generated by the model, while the predicted variable (path validity classification) is a final decision derived from the model's output layer. These are distinct stages in the inference pipeline; the final classification is not mechanically derived from the intermediate coordinates in a way that guarantees the result (the model must still learn to map those coordinates to the correct validity decision). The test set configurations are also generated independently of the training set, ensuring no data leakage creates a false correlation.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: concern

There is a risk that the result is predetermined by domain knowledge: if the task is purely geometric (grid-world paths), symbolic coordinates are theoretically sufficient, making a "positive" result (symbolic works) unsurprising to a robotics expert. Conversely, if the symbolic model fails significantly, it might be attributed to the specific architectural simplification (10M parameters) rather than the representational modality itself. A null result (symbolic fails) would need to be carefully distinguished from "symbolic representation is insufficient" vs. "the specific lightweight model implementation was too weak," which could make the finding less publishable if the distinction isn't razor-sharp.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: concern

The question names a domain relationship (structure vs. dimensionality in reasoning), which is good, but it is heavily qualified by implementation constraints ("minimal... required," "compared to high-dimensional"). A strict reading suggests the question is "Can we make a small CPU model do this?" rather than "What is the fundamental requirement for spatial generalization?" The focus on "minimal complexity" and the specific comparison to "high-dimensional learned embeddings" (a specific existing method) slightly narrows the scope to a method-evaluation context rather than a broader inquiry into the mechanisms of spatial intelligence.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validator_revise

The project addresses a valuable gap, but the research question is currently framed too closely around the specific implementation constraints (CPU, minimal size) and the direct comparison to a specific baseline (IPT), which risks making the answer a simple benchmark report. To ensure the question is a robust scientific inquiry about representation rather than a feasibility study, it should be reframed to isolate the *mechanism* of spatial generalization from the *efficiency* constraints.
