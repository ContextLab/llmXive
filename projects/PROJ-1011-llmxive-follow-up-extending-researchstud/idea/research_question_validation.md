## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass

The question asks about the universality of specific epistemic heuristics (ideation patterns) across distinct scientific cultures (ML vs. non-ML domains). While the methodology uses LLMs to test this, the core inquiry is about the transferability of research design principles, not the performance metrics of the LLM itself.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor (application of ML-derived patterns) and the predicted variable (expert ratings of proposal quality in non-ML domains) rely on independent data sources. The patterns are extracted from ML abstracts, while the evaluation is performed by human experts on non-ML proposals, ensuring the relationship is empirical rather than mechanically guaranteed by shared signal construction.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass

Both outcomes are highly informative: a positive result would suggest a universal grammar of scientific innovation, while a negative result would highlight critical domain-specific constraints in research design that AI tools currently fail to capture. A null result (no difference) would also be significant, indicating the patterns are too abstract to be actionable, which is a non-trivial finding for tool developers.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass

The question explicitly names a relationship between a specific set of heuristics and their applicability in a different domain, rather than focusing on computational constraints like runtime, model size, or specific hardware capabilities. The mention of resource constraints in the methodology supports the feasibility of the test but does not define the research question itself.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated

All four checks pass; the research question targets a substantive scientific phenomenon regarding the universality of research ideation patterns, avoids circular reasoning, and offers informative results regardless of the outcome. The project is ready to advance to initialization.
