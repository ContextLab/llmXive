## Unresolved concerns

- The plan uses Pearson correlation between shift vectors (derived from mean embeddings via $W_U^{+}\times f$) and WALS typological differences to infer that the edge‑spectrum encodes language‑specific priors. Correlation is purely associative; it does not establish that the edge‑spectrum *causes* the typological variation, yet the specification’s objective phrasing (“determine whether … encodes a universal … or shifts …”) implies a causal claim. The methodology therefore lacks a design (e.g., intervention, ablation) needed for causal validity.
- Frequency vectors are sourced from language‑filtered Common Crawl subsets, but the plan does not control for corpus‑level confounds (domain, register, data quality) that may drive differences in $f$ rather than true typological effects. Without matching or normalizing across corpora, the construct validity of the ‘mean embedding’ as a measure of linguistic prior is questionable.
- Models differ in architecture, training data, and tokenization. Although a paired‑architecture control (FR‑028) is mentioned, the plan lacks a detailed procedure for isolating the effect of language typology from these model‑level confounds (e.g., using multiple instances of the same architecture trained on monolingual vs. multilingual data). This insufficient confound control threatens the internal validity of any observed similarity differences.
- Bootstrap resampling is performed by resampling rows of the $U$ matrix (edge spectrum). This treats singular vectors as independent observations, which they are not; singular vectors are orthogonal components of a single matrix. Resampling rows may underestimate variance, leading to overly narrow confidence intervals and inflated significance claims.
- The permutation test combines three null distributions (within‑language, across‑model, model‑specific) but does not specify how they are weighted or whether they are mutually exclusive. Mixing heterogeneous nulls without a clear hierarchical testing strategy can inflate Type I error rates, compromising the validity of the p‑value.
- Functional Requirement FR‑016 (conduct correlation analyses on at least ten languages) is absent from the plan. No phase lists FR‑016, so the requirement lacks a corresponding implementation task.
- FR-006 requires verified URLs for language‑filtered Common Crawl subsets (≥ 1 M tokens per language). The plan explicitly states that no verified URLs are available and proceeds with a placeholder abort step. Without these datasets the pipeline cannot compute frequency distributions, mean embeddings, or any downstream analyses, making the approach infeasible. Supply verified dataset URLs (or an alternative verified corpus) before the plan can be accepted.
- FR-014 states that `edge_spectrum.json` must contain fields `model_pairs` and `similarity_matrix`, but `edge_spectrum.schema.yaml` defines only `model_name`, `top_k`, `edge_vectors`, `checksum`, and `generated_at`. This mismatch means the artifact cannot both satisfy the functional requirement and the JSON‑Schema contract.
- The plan describes generating bootstrap confidence intervals but never mentions creating an artifact that validates against `contracts/bootstrap_test.schema.yaml`. The contract exists but the plan does not exercise it, leaving a gap between the data model and the implementation plan.
- While the plan references `permutation_test.json`, it does not explicitly state that a `similarity_metric.json` artifact (required by `contracts/similarity_metric.schema.yaml`) will be produced. This omission could lead to missing contract validation for similarity metrics.
- The validation target (WALS typological feature differences) is not independent of the predictor (shift vectors derived from language‑specific token frequency distributions). Both the shift vectors (computed as W_U^{+} × f) and the WALS features are driven by the same underlying language‑specific token frequencies, making the correlation analysis circular and guaranteeing a non‑trivial relationship by construction.
- Edge‑spectrum subspaces are extracted per model, not per language. Yet the plan measures “cosine similarity between edge‑spectrum bases across model‑language pairs”, conflating model differences with language differences. Since the edge spectrum is a property of the model’s unembedding matrix (trained on multilingual data), it is not inherently language‑specific, so this comparison does not validly test the hypothesis of a universal vs. language‑specific prior.
- The plan treats the edge‑spectrum similarity metric and the mean‑embedding shift metric as independent predictors, but both are derived from the same unembedding matrix W_U. This violates the assumption that validation targets must be independent of the predictors, potentially inflating apparent effects and undermining the statistical interpretation of subsequent correlation and permutation tests.
