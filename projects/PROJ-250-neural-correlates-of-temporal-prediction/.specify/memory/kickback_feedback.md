## Unresolved concerns

- The plan identifies a critical gap: the primary dataset (ds000246) and verified alternatives lack the required 'complex' auditory scene condition. The proposed fallback to 'synthetic simulation' (research.md) to demonstrate pipeline functionality is methodologically unsound for the project's core research question. Synthetic data cannot validate the hypothesis that 'prediction error signatures scale with scene complexity' because the 'complexity' variable is artificially generated, not empirically observed. This renders the study unable to answer its own research question.
- The plan proposes a paired t-test comparing 'simple' vs. 'complex' conditions. However, the research.md admits the 'complex' condition may be missing or heuristically mapped. If the 'complex' condition is derived via heuristic (FR-007) or simulation, the assumption of a valid within-subject pairing is violated. A paired t-test requires two measurements from the *same* subject under two *valid* conditions. If one condition is synthetic or heuristically inferred, the statistical test loses causal/associational validity regarding the specific construct of 'auditory scene complexity'.
- The assumption that 'a heuristic fallback... is sufficient to approximate the condition' (spec.md) is scientifically invalid for testing neural correlates of complexity. Heuristics (e.g., mapping 'deviant' to 'complex') conflate stimulus probability with scene complexity, introducing a massive confound. The analysis would measure the effect of probability (oddball) rather than complexity, failing construct validity for the 'complex auditory scene' variable.
- SC-004 requires measuring 'Topographic consistency... against a canonical MMN topography template... using Pearson correlation coefficient (r ≥ 0.8)'. The plan.md, research.md, and data-model.md contain no mention of generating, storing, or comparing against a 'canonical template', nor do they define the calculation of a Pearson correlation coefficient for topography. The plan only mentions generating 'scalp topography maps' (visualize.py) but lacks the specific metric and comparison logic required by SC-004.
- SC-003 requires measuring effect size 'against established benchmarks for MMN effect sizes in literature (e.g., d ≥ 0.5)'. The plan.md and research.md mention calculating Cohen's d (FR-005) but do not include a step to compare the calculated value against a specific literature benchmark or threshold to determine 'practical significance'. The plan stops at reporting the value, not validating it against the criterion in SC-003.
- SC-001 requires measuring 'MMN amplitude difference... against the baseline noise floor in the pre-stimulus period'. While the plan mentions baseline correction (-200 to 0 ms) in research.md, it does not explicitly define a step to calculate the 'noise floor' (e.g., standard deviation of the baseline) and compare the MMN amplitude against it to ensure 'signal validity' as a distinct metric or pass/fail check.
- The plan cites OpenNeuro ds000246 as the primary dataset for investigating 'simple oddball' vs 'complex auditory scene' conditions. However, ds000246 (Auditory Oddball) is a canonical dataset containing ONLY a simple oddball paradigm (standard vs deviant tones). It does NOT contain a 'complex auditory scene' condition required by the research question (FR-008, US-2). Relying on this dataset makes the core hypothesis untestable as written. The plan must either identify a dataset that actually contains both conditions (e.g., a specific complex auditory scene dataset) or explicitly define how the 'complex' condition will be synthesized/simulated in the data generation phase, rather than assuming it exists in ds000246.
- The fallback strategy proposes using 'neurofusion/eeg-restingstate' and applying a 'heuristic mapping' to create 'simple/complex' conditions. Resting-state data lacks the structured stimulus events (standard/deviant) required to calculate Mismatch Negativity (MMN). MMN is defined by the difference between responses to deviant and standard stimuli; without these specific event labels in the data, MMN cannot be computed. This fallback is scientifically invalid for the stated research question and will result in a pipeline that runs but produces meaningless metrics.

- The plan proposes a 'heuristic fallback' (FR-007) to map 'deviant' trials to 'complex' conditions if 'condition_label' is missing. This creates a circular/tautological validation: the 'complex' condition is defined by the presence of deviant stimuli in the heuristic, yet the analysis tests if 'complex' conditions elicit larger MMN than 'simple' conditions. If 'simple' is defined by 'standard' trials and 'complex' by 'deviant' trials, the analysis merely confirms that deviant trials differ from standard trials (the definition of MMN), rather than testing if *scene complexity* modulates this effect. The predictor (stimulus type) and the validation target (condition label) are not independent.

If the dataset only contains standard/deviant trials without a separate 'complex scene' manipulation (e.g., background noise, multiple streams), the research question 'do prediction errors scale with scene complexity' is untestable with this data. The plan risks validating a null hypothesis by definition if the 'complex' condition is not an independent experimental variable.
- The plan uses a paired t-test comparing MMN metrics between 'simple' and 'complex' conditions. If the 'complex' condition is derived via the heuristic (mapping deviants to complex), the 'simple' condition (standards) and 'complex' condition (deviants) are not independent groups of trials in the way required for a complexity comparison. A valid test for complexity effects requires two distinct experimental conditions (e.g., Simple Oddball vs. Complex Scene), each containing both standard and deviant trials, to compare the *magnitude* of the MMN (Deviant-Standard) across the two contexts. The current design conflates stimulus type with condition complexity.
