## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: fail

The question is framed around the performance of a specific architectural modification ("token-sparse regime") on a specific model ("autoregressive video world models") rather than a fundamental scientific property of the world being simulated. While it asks about "degradation," the phenomenon of interest is actually the model's robustness to a specific engineering constraint, making the answer a benchmark result (e.g., "30% is the limit") rather than a discovery about physical consistency or causal dynamics independent of the method.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor variable is the "sparsity level" (an externally imposed constraint on the model's update mechanism), and the predicted variable is "drift error" (the deviation between model output and a ground-truth physics engine). Since the ground truth is generated by a separate, deterministic physics engine (Box2D) and is mathematically independent of the generative model's latent token updates, there is no circular construction.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: concern

While identifying a specific threshold is useful for engineering, the qualitative result that "increasing sparsity increases error" is almost predetermined by domain knowledge regarding information bandwidth and autoregressive error accumulation. If the null result (no degradation until total collapse) were found, it would be surprising, but the positive result (error grows with sparsity) is expected; thus, the question may lack the surprising binary or non-monotonic nature required for a high-impact scientific finding unless a specific, counter-intuitive phase transition is hypothesized.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: fail

The question names a constraint on the implementation ("token-sparse regime," "subset of latent tokens") as the primary variable of interest, rather than asking how a specific physical phenomenon behaves under uncertainty or limited information. It asks "how does the model degrade" rather than "what is the minimum information required to maintain causal consistency in a physical system," which would be a domain question.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validator_revise

What is the minimum information bandwidth (in latent tokens per step) required to maintain causal consistency and physical stability in autoregressive simulations of non-linear dynamic systems, and does a phase transition exist where error accumulation shifts from linear to exponential?
