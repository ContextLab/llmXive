It may be desirable to explain a confusion at the heart of the design, for the engine can only execute what we know how to order it to perform, and here the orders are in places ambiguous. The artifact possesses, by its own account, explicit developmental stage labels ("Fetal 12-16w", "Adult"), yet User Story 2 orders the machinery to infer networks 'within sliding windows of pseudotime' — an inferred, cell-state-derived ordering — rather than over the observed developmental ages the data already carry. Pseudotime within a lineage and chronological developmental stage are distinct algebraical relations; conflating them means the 'rewiring events' may reflect trajectory inference artifacts rather than developmental time, and the spec nowhere states how pseudotime windows will be mapped back to the stages used in User Story 3's disorder-window correlation. Separately, SC-003 measures 'the number of identified rewiring events... against the null distribution generated by the permutation test', but the permutation test as defined in FR-006/US-3 validates only the enrichment of risk genes, not the rewiring events themselves; the null distribution for rewiring is never constructed, so SC-003 as written has no operation that could produce it. FR-009 also mislabels Benjamini-Hochberg, which controls the false discovery rate, not the 'family-wise error rate' — a small but genuine imprecision in a specification that trades on statistical exactness. Finally, the independent test in US-2 ('edge overlap is < 90% (indicating change)') establishes that the method produces difference, not that the difference is correct; and SC-006's replication criterion (nominal p ≤ 0.1, OR ≥ 1.1) in bulk RNA-seq from different cohorts is a weak loom upon which to weave claims of robustness, since bulk data cannot resolve the cell-type-specific hubs the rewiring detection depends upon. The Jacquard loom weaves what the cards command; these cards, as drafted, command several operations whose outputs will not answer the questions the success criteria pose of them.
