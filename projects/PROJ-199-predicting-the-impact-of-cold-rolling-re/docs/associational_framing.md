# Associational Framing Policy

This document establishes the policy for interpreting and reporting results from the predictive models in this project.

## Core Principle: Association, Not Causation
All findings generated by this pipeline are **associational**. The models identify statistical relationships between input variables (cold rolling reduction, material type) and output variables (texture descriptors).

## Rationale
1. **Observational Data**: The training data is derived from observational EBSD measurements, not controlled experiments where all other variables are held constant.
2. **Confounding Variables**: Unmeasured factors (e.g., grain size, temperature, initial defects) may influence both the reduction and the resulting texture. The models cannot distinguish between direct effects and confounding effects.
3. **Model Nature**: The regression models (Polynomial, Gaussian Process) are mathematical functions that map inputs to outputs. They do not encode physical laws or causal mechanisms.

## Reporting Guidelines
When reporting results from this pipeline:

### DO:
- Use language such as "associated with," "correlated with," "predicts," "trend," "relationship."
- Example: "The Brass component volume fraction is **positively associated** with cold rolling reduction in Aluminum."
- Example: "The model **predicts** an increase in the Copper component as reduction increases."
- Reference the "Physics Check" module as a validation of consistency with known metallurgical trends, not as proof of causality.

### DO NOT:
- Use language such as "causes," "leads to," "results in," "drives," "mechanism."
- Example (Incorrect): "Cold rolling reduction **causes** the formation of the Brass texture."
- Example (Incorrect): "The model **proves** that reduction drives the increase in Brass component."

## The Role of Physics Checks
The `code/analysis/physics_check.py` module validates that the observed trends are **consistent** with known physical principles (e.g., FCC metals typically develop Brass texture with rolling).
- **Validation**: Passing the physics check confirms that the association is physically plausible.
- **Limitation**: It does not prove that the model has discovered the underlying physical mechanism. It merely ensures the association is not an artifact of data noise or model error.

## Handling Anomalies
If the model identifies an association that contradicts known physics (e.g., Brass component decreasing with reduction in a standard FCC metal):
1. **Flag**: The sample is flagged as "deviant" by `code/analysis/texture_validation.py`.
2. **Investigation**: This may indicate a data quality issue, an anomalous material behavior, or a limitation in the model.
3. **Reporting**: Report the anomaly as an "observed deviation from standard trends" rather than a "failure of the physical law."

## Implications for Decision Making
- **Prediction**: The models can be used to predict texture descriptors for new reduction levels within the trained range.
- **Optimization**: Caution should be exercised when using the models for process optimization. Optimizing for a predicted texture does not guarantee the physical outcome if confounding variables are not controlled.
- **Scientific Inquiry**: The models should be used to generate hypotheses about texture evolution, which must then be tested with controlled experiments.

## Compliance
All automated reports generated by `code/analysis/physics_check.py` and `code/models/train.py` explicitly include a disclaimer stating the associational nature of the findings. Users are expected to adhere to this framing in any external publications or reports derived from this project.
