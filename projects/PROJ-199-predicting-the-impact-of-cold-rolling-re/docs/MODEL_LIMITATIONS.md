# Model Limitations and Physical Constraints

## Executive Summary

The predictive models in this project identify statistical associations between cold-rolling reduction and texture evolution in FCC metals. **These models are not causal models** and should not be used to infer physical mechanisms without additional experimental validation.

## Detailed Limitations

### 1. Missing Physical Variables

The current feature set includes:
- Cold-rolling reduction (%)
- Material type (Al, Cu, Ni)

**Excluded variables that influence texture evolution**:
- **Grain Size**: Initial and evolving grain size affects dislocation mean free path and texture development rates.
- **Stacking Fault Energy (SFE)**: SFE determines the dominant deformation mechanism (slip vs. twinning), which strongly influences texture components.
- **Dislocation Density**: The evolution of dislocation structures drives texture hardening and component intensity.
- **Alloy Composition**: Minor alloying elements can significantly alter texture evolution paths.
- **Temperature**: Cold rolling temperature (even within "cold" range) affects recovery rates and texture stability.

**Impact**: The models capture the aggregate effect of these variables through the material type and reduction level, but cannot disentangle their individual contributions. Predictions for materials with unusual SFE or grain size distributions may be inaccurate.

### 2. Symmetry and Crystallographic Constraints

- **Re-indexing**: Orientations are re-indexed to FCC symmetry (T018) before descriptor calculation. This ensures consistency but does not enforce symmetry on **predictions**.
- **Component Classification**: Volume fractions are calculated using MTEX-style search algorithms with fixed Euler ranges. These ranges may not capture all variants for non-standard textures.
- **Mass Balance**: The sum of Brass, Copper, S, Goss, and "random" fractions is constrained to 1.0 ± 0.01 (T019). Predictions violating this constraint are flagged and excluded.

**Risk**: Predictions outside the training distribution may yield physically impossible texture states (e.g., negative volume fractions, sum ≠ 1.0).

### 3. Extrapolation and Domain Shift

- **Training Range**: Reduction levels typically span 0-80%. Predictions outside this range are flagged as extrapolated (T028).
- **Confidence Penalty**: Extrapolated predictions receive a confidence penalty factor applied to the standard error.
- **Material Generalization**: Models trained on Al, Cu, and Ni may not generalize to other FCC metals (e.g., Ag, Au, Pb) without retraining.

**Recommendation**: Do not use the models for reduction levels < 0% or > 80% without explicit validation.

### 4. Data Quality and Selection Bias

- **Confidence Threshold**: Points with confidence index < 0.1 are filtered. This may exclude regions of high deformation or poor EBSD quality, introducing bias.
- **Exclusion Logic**: Samples with >50% filtered points are excluded (T014). This may remove extreme cases (e.g., very high reduction, poor texture) from the dataset.
- **Trend Deviation**: Samples deviating from standard FCC trends are flagged (T022) but retained. These outliers may represent novel physics or measurement errors.

**Impact**: The training dataset may be biased toward "well-behaved" samples, potentially underestimating the variability of texture evolution.

### 5. Model-Specific Limitations

#### Polynomial Regression (Degree=2)
- **Assumption**: Texture evolution follows a quadratic trend with reduction.
- **Limitation**: Cannot capture complex non-monotonic behavior or plateaus.
- **Risk**: Overfitting at high reduction levels if data is sparse.

#### Gaussian Process (RBF Kernel)
- **Assumption**: Smooth, continuous texture evolution.
- **Limitation**: May oversmooth sharp transitions or phase-like changes in texture.
- **Risk**: Computationally expensive for large datasets; hyperparameter sensitivity.

## Associational Framing

All results, reports, and visualizations generated by this project explicitly frame findings as **associational relationships**. Key phrases used:
- "associated with"
- "correlated with"
- "predicted by"
- "statistical relationship"

**Avoid**:
- "causes"
- "drives"
- "mechanism of"
- "due to" (without qualification)

## Validation and Uncertainty Quantification

- **Cross-Validation**: K-fold CV (T027) provides R² and RMSE estimates.
- **Sensitivity Analysis**: Tolerance sweeps assess robustness (T033, T034).
- **Physics Check**: Trend validation against known FCC behavior (T029).
- **Variance Decomposition**: Shapley values quantify missing variable impact (T035, T036).

**Uncertainty Reporting**:
- All predictions include confidence intervals (± 2σ for GP models).
- Extrapolated predictions carry additional penalty factors.
- Outlier samples are flagged in reports.

## Recommendations for Future Work

1. **Include Microstructural Features**: Integrate grain size, SFE, and dislocation density as input features.
2. **Expand Material Scope**: Train on additional FCC metals to improve generalization.
3. **Dynamic Symmetry Constraints**: Enforce physical constraints (e.g., non-negative fractions) directly in the model architecture.
4. **Causal Inference**: Use experimental designs (e.g., controlled SFE variations) to isolate causal mechanisms.
5. **Uncertainty-Aware Training**: Incorporate measurement uncertainty into the loss function.
