## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass

The question asks about a fundamental property of the teleoperation control loop: the specific information content (geometric vs. textural) required for a policy to generalize from simulation to reality. While the proposal mentions specific methods like GRUs and YOLO-Nano, these are framed as tools to isolate the variable of "fidelity" rather than as the subject of the inquiry itself; the core question remains about the relationship between visual abstraction and Sim2Real robustness.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor variable is derived from the "sparse latent vectors" (object centroids, kinematic states) generated by a perception module, while the predicted outcome is the "task success rate" measured in a physics simulator (PyBullet) against ground-truth task definitions. These are independent data sources; the success metric is not a mathematical transformation of the latent vector but an empirical evaluation of the policy's ability to manipulate objects in a physics engine.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass

Both outcomes are highly informative: a finding that high-fidelity video is strictly necessary would define a hard hardware floor for edge robotics, while a finding that sparse latents suffice would democratize teleoperation by enabling CPU-only fleets. The existence of a "fidelity cliff" is not predetermined by current domain knowledge, as the specific trade-off curve between pixel-level detail and control performance in this specific digital teleoperation context remains unquantified.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass

The question explicitly names a domain relationship ("minimum visual fidelity threshold" vs. "Sim2Real transfer") rather than an implementation constraint. It seeks to understand the limits of information retention in robotic perception, not merely whether a specific model can run within a specific budget; the budget constraints are secondary to the scientific inquiry about what features matter for transfer.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated

All four checks pass, as the research question targets a genuine, unquantified gap in the relationship between visual representation fidelity and robotic policy transfer. The proposal correctly identifies a scientific variable (fidelity threshold) rather than an engineering benchmark, and the methodology is designed to empirically map the trade-off curve without circular dependencies. The project is ready for initialization.
