## Research-question validation

### Phenomenon-vs-method check

**Verdict**: pass

The question asks about a substantive psychological mechanism: how an individual difference (trait anxiety) modulates the causal link between a specific attentional behavior (gaze fixation) and a memory outcome (recall accuracy) under high temporal load. It does not frame the inquiry around the performance of a specific algorithm, hardware constraint, or software implementation, but rather investigates a domain relationship in cognitive psychology.

### Circularity check

**Verdict**: pass

The predictor (gaze fixation duration) is derived from eye-tracking hardware data, while the predicted variable (recall accuracy) is derived from independent post-task behavioral responses. Although both are influenced by the stimulus, they are not derived from the same mathematical summary of a single signal; fixation is a measure of temporal allocation, while recall is a measure of successful encoding, making the relationship empirically testable rather than mechanically guaranteed.

### Triviality check

**Verdict**: pass

Both positive and null results are scientifically informative. A positive interaction would confirm that anxiety enhances the translation of attention into memory for threat cues even under load, supporting "enhanced encoding" models. Conversely, a null result would be highly significant, suggesting that the rapid presentation load disrupts the consolidation of threat-biased attention, thereby challenging the assumption that attentional capture always leads to better memory in anxiety disorders.

### Question-narrowing check

**Verdict**: pass

The question explicitly names a relationship between three psychological constructs (anxiety, fixation duration, and recall) within a specific experimental context (rapid sequences). It avoids implementation constraints such as "can we achieve X accuracy with Y algorithm" and instead asks "how does X modulate the relationship between Y and Z," which is a classic domain inquiry.

### Overall verdict

**Verdict**: validated

All four checks pass; the research question targets a genuine gap in the literature regarding the interplay of anxiety, attention, and memory without falling into circular logic or implementation-focused framing. The proposed three-way interaction is a well-defined psychological hypothesis that can be tested with the described methodology.
